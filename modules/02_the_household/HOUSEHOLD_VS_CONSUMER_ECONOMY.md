# Household Economy vs Consumer Economy

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields

---

## Two economic imaginations

| Dimension | Household Economy | Consumer Economy |
|---|---|---|
| Center of life | Household, land, and relationships | Marketplace and individual preferences |
| Unit of economic life | Household (producers and consumers together) | Individual consumer |
| Relationship to production | Households produce much of what they consume | Households consume what others produce |
| Economic motive | Stewardship, provision, gift, and sufficiency | Profit, growth, and preference satisfaction |
| Relationship to land | Land is a covenantal partner; cared for and rested | Land is a resource to be extracted |
| Time horizon | Generational; inheritance; Jubilee | Quarterly; investment return; obsolescence |
| Measure of wealth | Flourishing households, productive land, strong community fabric | GDP, income, consumption volume |
| Relationship to neighbor | Gleaning, shared commons, mutual aid | Competition, price signals, market transactions |
| Formation produced | Patience, skill, stewardship, dependency on creation | Convenience, preference, consumption habits, restlessness |

## Theological diagnosis

Consumer economics trains imagination to treat:

- persons as choosers
- goods as satisfactions
- communities as marketplaces

That vision can make every relationship transactional. Christian formation is then deformed at the root because covenant obligations are replaced by preference management.

## The inversion moment (brief history)

- **Industrial Revolution:** production moved out of households and into factories
- **20th century mass consumption:** consumption consolidated in malls and centralized retail
- **Digital platform age:** even relationships were increasingly mediated as market interactions

The result was not only economic change but formation change.

## Reordering without market denial

Covenantal household economics is not anti-market. It is pre-market.

Markets can serve good ends, but they must remain derivative. The household forms people first, then participates in exchange from that formed posture.

Practical reordering includes:

- producing more at household scale where possible
- buying from local producers when household production is limited
- treating table, hospitality, and generosity as economic acts
- refusing growth logic that destroys household and land rhythms

## Gleaning as Hebraic economic grammar

Leviticus 19:9–10 and Ruth 2 establish a crucial principle: harvest was never entirely private. The edges of the field belonged to the poor and the stranger.

Private productivity therefore had a built-in communal claim.

This is covenantal economics in seed form: ownership bounded by mercy.

## Formation question for households

What habits is your household currently training?

- speed or patience?
- convenience or skill?
- isolation or hospitality?
- private accumulation or shared provision?

As you walk in this, reorder one economic habit at a time.
