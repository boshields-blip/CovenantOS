# Household Flow Model

**Status:** Draft | **Owner:** @boshields-blip | **Last updated:** 2026-07-31

---

## The flow

```text
Land & Creation
    ↓
Work & Craft
    ↓
Garden & Provision
    ↓
Household Table
    ↓
Hospitality
    ↓
Local Trade & Exchange
    ↓
Community & Commons
    ↓
(back to Land & Creation)
```

This is not a productivity pipeline. It is a formation cycle.

## 1) Land & Creation

Everything begins with received creation, not manufactured autonomy. Soil, water, weather, creatures, and limits are givens. The household does not invent its world; it stewards it.

## 2) Work & Craft

Labor applied to creation turns possibility into provision. Craft is where skill, patience, and discipline are learned over time. In Hebraic terms, this is *avodah*: work and worship held together.

## 3) Garden & Provision

Food and material goods come through cultivation and care. Even when households buy from others, this stage reminds them that real goods come from land, labor, and time — never from pure abstraction.

## 4) Household Table

The table is where provision becomes formation: gratitude, story, correction, reconciliation, blessing, and shared memory. Consumption is not only caloric intake; it is covenant practice. See [Come to the Table philosophy](../01_the_word/05_formation_layer/come_to_the_table/PHILOSOPHY.md).

## 5) Hospitality

The table opens outward. Neighbor, stranger, friend, and church are welcomed into household life. Hospitality converts private abundance into public mercy.

## 6) Local Trade & Exchange

Surplus and specialties move through local exchange: market stands, skilled services, shared tools, trusted trades. Trade is real and good when it serves households and place instead of dissolving them.

## 7) Community & Commons

Repeated exchange and hospitality build commons: trust, mutual aid, remembered obligation, and shared places. Ruth's gleaning scene is a classic pattern — private fields with public edges (Ruth 2; Leviticus 19:9–10).

## Why the flow is circular, not linear

Linear systems often extract and discard. Covenant household flow renews and returns:

- soil is rested
- tools are repaired
- animals are tended
- people are fed and formed
- relationships are reinforced

The end of one cycle is preparation for the next. This grammar aligns with Sabbath and Jubilee logic: production is bounded by renewal, not optimized into exhaustion.

## Formation at every stage

Each stage forms people:

- land teaches creaturely dependence
- work teaches patience and skill
- provision teaches gratitude
- table teaches belonging
- hospitality teaches generosity
- trade teaches fairness and accountability
- commons teaches shared responsibility

## Contrast: consumer flow

The consumer model often begins at purchase and ends at disposal. It bypasses land, craft, hospitality, and commons. People become choosers, goods become satisfactions, and neighbors become competitors.

The household model begins before purchase and continues after use, because its aim is not only getting goods but becoming a people.

## Hebraic anchors

- **Proverbs 31** — household diligence, trade, and care intertwined
- **Ruth 2 + Leviticus 19:9–10** — gleaning as built-in commons
- **Sabbath** — weekly boundary against endless extraction
- **Jubilee (Leviticus 25)** — long-cycle restoration of land and households

## Agrarian rhythm and Piedmont practice

In the Piedmont of South Carolina, this cycle has a recognizable texture: spring planting, summer tending, hay cutting windows, fall harvest and preservation, winter mending and planning. A household with pasture, animals, and a kitchen garden can practice this flow while still participating in local market exchange and neighbor care.

Formation happens in the doing.
