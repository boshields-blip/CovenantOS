# Land and Inheritance

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields

---

## Why land cannot be background

The Hebrew word *eretz* means land, earth, ground. It is one of the most frequent words in the Hebrew Bible. In Scripture, land is never mere scenery. It is covenant territory where obedience, justice, memory, and inheritance take shape.

## Land as covenant participant

In the Abrahamic promise, three strands are held together: seed, land, and blessing. Land is not an accessory to covenant life; it is part of the covenant frame itself.

This aligns with the governing architecture in [covenantal_philosophy.md](/the-word/the-land-of-the-text): covenant is embodied in time, people, and place.

## Promised land theology

Scripture's land vision resists absolute ownership claims:

- land is **given**, not earned
- land is **stewarded**, not possessed without limit
- land can "vomit out" inhabitants when defiled (Leviticus 18:28)
- land "mourns" when justice collapses (Hosea 4:3)

Land responds to covenant faithfulness and covenant violation.

## Jubilee and the anti-alienation principle

Leviticus 25's Jubilee legislation declares that land returns to the original family allotments every fifty years. This is a radical boundary on permanent alienation.

The principle is clear:

- ultimate ownership belongs to God
- penultimate holding belongs to families in trust
- market transactions are bounded by covenant restoration

## Modern contrast

Modern land tenure often treats land as commodity:

- financialized assets detached from place
- real estate as investment class
- ownership by absentee entities rather than resident stewards

That pattern can separate household from land, and inheritance from responsibility.

## *Bayit* and *eretz* together

Hebraic thought pairs house and land: *bayit* and *eretz*. A household without relation to place becomes abstract; land without household stewardship becomes exploitable.

Covenantal life keeps them joined.

## Inheritance as covenant obligation

Inheritance is not merely retirement planning. It is covenant continuity. Proverbs 13:22 names a multigenerational responsibility: "A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children."

What is passed on includes more than money:

- productive patterns
- stories and memory
- skills and tools
- soil health and place attachment
- neighborly trust

## Piedmont South Carolina context

In the Piedmont, land carries a layered history and a present vocation. To hold even a small piece of ground is to receive a concrete stewardship: pasture care, garden discipline, watershed attention, fence and tree maintenance, and local-market responsibility.

The opportunity is not to idolize region, but to practice covenant fidelity in a real place.

## Formation questions

- What land has been entrusted to your household?
- Do you treat it as possession or stewardship?
- What practices are restoring or depleting it?
- What inheritance are you preparing to pass on?
- What must change now for your grandchildren to bless God on the same ground?
