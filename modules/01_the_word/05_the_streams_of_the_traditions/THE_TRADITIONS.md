# The Traditions

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields | **Last updated:** 2026-08-01

---

Each stream below is mapped the same way: its center of gravity, its genuine gifts, the blind spots it tends to carry, and what any believer can receive from it. Gifts come first, always — not as flattery, but because every one of these streams has produced saints and wisdom, and you cannot rightly weigh what you have not first honored. These are common tendencies, not verdicts on any individual. Real people are always more than their tradition.

For the lens CovenantOS itself reads from, see [The Household-Covenant Tradition](/the-word/the-streams-of-the-traditions/the-household-covenant-tradition).

---

## Reformed

**Center of gravity:** The sovereignty of God over all of life, salvation by grace alone, and a high view of Scripture worked out in careful, systematic theology.

**Gifts:** Remarkable theological depth and coherence. A God-centered vision that dignifies all of life as lived before God (*coram Deo*). Serious, disciplined engagement with Scripture. A strong sense that ideas matter and that the whole of reality holds together under God. It has produced some of the church's clearest thinkers and most durable confessions.

**Blind spots to watch:** Theological precision can become the main proof of maturity, leaving embodiment, relationship, and ordinary covenant practice underdeveloped. Debate can crowd out worship and mercy. Certainty can harden into a combative posture toward those who see differently.

**What to receive:** That God is God over everything, and that thinking carefully about Him is an act of love, not a distraction from it.

## Baptist

**Center of gravity:** A regenerate church of believers, entered by personal faith and marked by believer's baptism; the authority of Scripture and the freedom of the local congregation.

**Gifts:** A clear call to personal conversion and discipleship. High regard for Scripture and for the local church as a real, accountable body. Deep commitment to religious liberty and to the conscience before God. Strong lay involvement and evangelistic energy.

**Blind spots to watch:** The stress on individual decision can slide toward individualism, treating faith as mainly a private transaction. Congregational independence can weaken the sense of the wider church and of historical rootedness. Sacraments can be reduced to bare symbols, and the deep formation of liturgy and tradition undervalued.

**What to receive:** That faith is personal and must be owned, and that the local congregation is a real covenant community, not an abstraction.

## Non-denominational evangelical

**Center of gravity:** Bible-centered, conversion-focused, culturally adaptive Christianity unbound from formal denominational structures.

**Gifts:** Accessibility and evangelistic reach. A genuine hunger to make faith understandable and lived. Flexibility and responsiveness to people who would never enter an older institution. A high value on personal relationship with Jesus and on Scripture.

**Blind spots to watch:** Without the ballast of tradition and confession, it can drift with the surrounding culture — importing consumer, therapeutic, and corporate assumptions without noticing. Novelty and relevance can quietly outrank faithfulness. Shallow historical roots can leave believers vulnerable to whatever is newest, and personality-driven leadership can go unaccountable.

**What to receive:** That the gospel must be spoken freshly and lived accessibly — and the reminder that freshness needs deep roots to stay faithful.

## Pentecostal & Charismatic

**Center of gravity:** The present, active power of the Holy Spirit — gifts, healing, prophecy, and immediate encounter with God.

**Gifts:** Expectancy that God acts now, not only in the past. Vibrant, embodied worship. Openness to the Spirit that resists the dryness of over-intellectualized faith. Care for the whole person. Extraordinary global growth and a faith that reaches the poor and the marginalized.

**Blind spots to watch:** Inner experience can become too authoritative, unmoored from Scripture and the testing of the wider church. Emotional intensity can be mistaken for spiritual depth. Teaching can tilt toward triumphalism or prosperity, and disappointment can be read as personal failure of faith.

**What to receive:** That God is living and active and pours out His Spirit on ordinary people — and that the spiritual life is meant to be felt, not only understood.

## Roman Catholic

**Center of gravity:** The visible, historic church; the sacraments as real means of grace; the unity of Scripture and tradition under a teaching authority.

**Gifts:** A deep sense of the church across time — that we are not the first Christians and will not be the last. Sacramental richness that takes the physical world seriously as a vehicle of grace. A vast heritage of worship, mysticism, and social teaching. A serious moral vision, especially on the dignity of life, the family, and the common good. Stability and continuity across centuries.

**Blind spots to watch:** The weight of institution and tradition can obscure the immediacy of grace and the accessibility of Scripture. Layers of mediation can distance ordinary believers from a direct life with God. Size and history bring the temptations of power and inertia.

**What to receive:** That the church is older, larger, and more physical than we imagine — a body across time, not a gathering we invented.

---

## Eastern Orthodox

**Center of gravity:** The worship and theology of the ancient undivided church; salvation as union with God (*theosis*); mystery guarded through liturgy and the Fathers.

**Gifts:** A profound sense of transcendence, beauty, and mystery. Continuity with the earliest church and its worship. A vision of salvation as transformation and participation in God's life, not merely a legal verdict. Deep spiritual disciplines and a resistance to reducing faith to information.

**Blind spots to watch:** Reverence for tradition and form can resist needed reform and make the faith hard to enter for outsiders. Emphasis on mystery can under-describe the plain, teachable content of the gospel. Ethnic and national identity can entangle the church.

**What to receive:** That salvation is union with a holy God, that worship is meant to be beautiful and reverent, and that mystery is not the enemy of truth.

---

## Anglican

**Center of gravity:** A "middle way" holding together Scripture, tradition, and reason; common prayer; a broad church of catholic and reformed instincts.

**Gifts:** The formative power of shared liturgy — the Book of Common Prayer shaping believers through repeated, beautiful, biblical words. A generous breadth that holds difference within one body. A sacramental yet Word-centered balance. A long tradition of thoughtful, pastoral faith.

**Blind spots to watch:** Breadth can become vagueness, and comprehensiveness can slide into a reluctance to say anything clearly. The via media can leave believers unsure what the church actually holds. Establishment and respectability can dull prophetic edge.

**What to receive:** That prayer shared in common, repeated over years, forms the soul more deeply than novelty ever can.

---

## Methodist & Wesleyan

**Center of gravity:** Grace that both saves and transforms; the pursuit of holiness of heart and life; faith organized into disciplined community and active mercy.

**Gifts:** A warm, personal experience of grace joined to serious discipleship. The genius of small, accountable groups (the class meeting) for real formation. A strong link between personal holiness and social mercy — care for the poor, the imprisoned, the enslaved. Confidence that grace actually changes people.

**Blind spots to watch:** The stress on experience and growth can tip into perfectionism or anxious self-measurement. Activism can outrun theological depth. Method can become mere machinery when the fire fades.

**What to receive:** That grace is meant to *transform*, not only pardon, and that holiness grows best in small, honest, accountable community.

---

## Lutheran

**Center of gravity:** Justification by faith alone; the sharp distinction of law and gospel; Christ given in Word and sacrament.

**Gifts:** The clearest guarding of grace against every attempt to earn God's favor. A pastoral theology that meets the guilty conscience with the pure comfort of the gospel. A rich sacramental sense that Christ is truly given, not merely remembered. Deep hymnody and a vocation-honoring view of ordinary work as holy.

**Blind spots to watch:** The law-gospel distinction, pressed too hard, can underplay the call to holiness and growth. Assurance can drift toward passivity. Confessional strength can turn inward and static.

**What to receive:** That the gospel is pure gift, that grace cannot be earned, and that ordinary work is a genuine calling before God.

---

## Anabaptist

**Center of gravity:** A visible community of committed disciples living the way of Jesus, marked by peace, simplicity, and separation from the coercive powers of the world.

**Gifts:** Costly, serious discipleship — following Jesus as a whole way of life, not only a belief. A strong communal ethic of mutual aid and shared life. A clear witness for peace and against the idolatry of power. Integrity between what is confessed and how life is actually lived.

**Blind spots to watch:** Separation from the world can become withdrawal and cultural isolation. The stress on the community's purity can turn legalistic or inward. Suspicion of all worldly power can under-engage legitimate responsibilities in the wider society.

**What to receive:** That following Jesus is a concrete way of life lived in community, and that the church is meant to look visibly different from the world.

## Also mapped

### Mainline Protestant

**Center of gravity:** Historic Protestant denominations marked by liturgical continuity, social conscience, and engagement with the modern world.

**Gifts:** A strong witness for justice and the dignity of the vulnerable, often speaking when others were silent. Liturgical and historical rootedness. A thoughtful willingness to engage hard questions honestly.

**Blind spots to watch:** Hope can be relocated from God's redemption to social and historical progress. The assumptions of the surrounding culture can reshape the gospel rather than being reshaped by it. Institutional decline can breed a survival mindset.

**What to receive:** That the gospel has public, social weight, and that faith must engage the real questions of its age.

### The Holiness movement

**Center of gravity:** A Wesleyan-rooted pursuit of entire sanctification — a heart wholly given to God — and a clean, consecrated life.

**Gifts:** A serious, hopeful expectation that God really can purify and empower a life. Warm devotion and moral earnestness. A history of mercy toward the poor and marginalized.

**Blind spots to watch:** The pursuit of purity can become perfectionism, legalism, or anxious introspection. Standards can multiply into external rule-keeping that misses the heart.

**What to receive:** That God intends real transformation of the heart, and that longing to be wholly His is not naïve but faithful.

### Restorationist (Stone-Campbell)

**Center of gravity:** The recovery of "New Testament Christianity" — restoring the beliefs and practices of the earliest church, and Christian unity on the Bible alone.

**Gifts:** A genuine hunger for the simplicity and pattern of the early church. High regard for Scripture and for weekly communion. A sincere heart for the unity of all Christians, refusing sectarian labels.

**Blind spots to watch:** The confidence that one has simply recovered the pure New Testament pattern can blind a tradition to its own assumptions and become its own kind of sectarianism. Restorationism can undervalue everything the Spirit did in the church between the apostles and now.

**What to receive:** The longing to recover the early church's pattern and simplicity — a longing the Household-Covenant tradition shares.

### Messianic Jewish

**Center of gravity:** Faith in Jesus (Yeshua) as Messiah lived within Jewish identity, practice, and the feasts, honoring the Hebraic roots of the faith.

**Gifts:** A living recovery of the Jewish context of Jesus and the early church. Attention to the feasts, the Sabbath, and the covenant story as one continuous whole. A vivid sense that the gospel did not fall from the sky but grew from the soil of Israel's covenant.

**Blind spots to watch:** Recovery of Jewish practice can tip toward a new legalism, or toward treating observance as a mark of superior faith. Identity questions can become fraught. Enthusiasm can outrun careful theology.

**What to receive:** That Jesus and the early church were thoroughly Jewish, and that reading Scripture in its Hebraic, covenantal context is not optional background but the native soil of the faith — a conviction close to the heart of CovenantOS.
