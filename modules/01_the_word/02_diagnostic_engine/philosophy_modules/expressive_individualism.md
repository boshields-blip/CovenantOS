## Name

**Module name:** Expressive Individualism

## Description

Expressive individualism imagines the true self as something inward, private, and original that must be discovered and publicly expressed. External structures are meaningful only insofar as they validate or enable the inner self.

## Category-by-category summary

### 1. Metaphysics

- Core claim: reality is most meaningful when it becomes a stage for personal authenticity.
- Diagnostic signal: repeated suspicion toward inherited forms, limits, and givenness.
- Notes: creation is treated less as order to receive and more as raw context for self-articulation.

### 2. Epistemology

- Core claim: the inner self has privileged access to truth about identity and purpose.
- Diagnostic signal: phrases such as "I need to be true to who I am" function as final authority.
- Notes: feeling and inward resonance often outrank communal wisdom or covenantal obligation.

### 3. Anthropology

- Core claim: a person is primarily an inner identity seeking outward congruence.
- Diagnostic signal: vocation, church, and family are treated as optional supports for self-realization.
- Notes: dependence and inheritance are often viewed as threats to freedom.

### 4. Ethics

- Core claim: the good is authentic self-expression without suppression.
- Diagnostic signal: moral language gives way to language of betrayal, repression, or authenticity.
- Notes: obedience is tolerated only if it feels self-chosen and self-affirming.

### 5. Social/Political

- Core claim: society should maximize space for private identity projects.
- Diagnostic signal: communities are evaluated mainly by affirmation rather than by covenantal truthfulness.
- Notes: institutions become legitimate only when they ratify the inner self.

### 6. Identity/Meaning

- Core claim: meaning comes from discovering and expressing the true self.
- Diagnostic signal: identity is narrated as journey, voice, platform, expression, and visibility.
- Notes: belonging is conditional on recognition rather than covenant fidelity.

### 7. Eschatology

- Core claim: the hoped-for future is one in which the self is finally unrestrained and fully seen.
- Diagnostic signal: salvation is imagined as personal congruence and social affirmation.
- Notes: hope becomes therapeutic authenticity instead of resurrectional renewal.

## Distortions this module often produces

- It treats received limits as enemies rather than as part of creaturely wisdom.
- It turns discipleship into self-expression with spiritual language added.
- It weakens covenantal belonging by making affirmation the condition of participation.

## Strengths or truths it often notices

- It correctly sees that hypocrisy and coercive conformity can deform the soul.
- It recognizes that persons are not machines and cannot flourish through sheer external pressure.
- It notices that institutions can suppress real wounds and real voices.

## Covenantal contrast

The covenantal philosophy agrees that persons matter and that false conformity is not faithfulness. But identity is received from God before it is expressed by the self. Freedom is not self-authorship; it is rightly ordered life before God, with and for others, in covenantal truth.
