## Name

**Module name:** Postmodernism

## Description

Postmodernism is less a single doctrine than a posture of suspicion toward totalizing narratives, stable foundations, and claims to universal neutrality. It highlights the role of power, language, location, and historical contingency in shaping knowledge.

## Category-by-category summary

### 1. Metaphysics

- Core claim: claims about stable reality are entangled with interpretation and power.
- Diagnostic signal: reluctance to affirm enduring order without extensive qualification.
- Notes: givenness can dissolve into discourse.

### 2. Epistemology

- Core claim: knowledge is perspectival, mediated, and often implicated in power structures.
- Diagnostic signal: truth claims are routinely met with genealogical suspicion.
- Notes: critique becomes easier than confidence.

### 3. Anthropology

- Core claim: the self is socially and linguistically constructed.
- Diagnostic signal: identity is narrated through location, discourse, and resistance.
- Notes: creaturely nature becomes difficult to name.

### 4. Ethics

- Core claim: moral language must account for power, exclusion, and context.
- Diagnostic signal: the first ethical move is often deconstruction.
- Notes: this can yield necessary humility but also moral paralysis.

### 5. Social/Political

- Core claim: institutions and narratives should be interrogated for hidden domination.
- Diagnostic signal: authority is presumed suspect until it proves otherwise.
- Notes: trust becomes thin and durable institutions become hard to sustain.

### 6. Identity/Meaning

- Core claim: identity is narrated, contested, and unstable.
- Diagnostic signal: inherited identities are approached with suspicion.
- Notes: the self becomes a site of perpetual revision.

### 7. Eschatology

- Core claim: grand futures are dangerous because they often conceal coercion.
- Diagnostic signal: hope is local, provisional, and anti-totalizing.
- Notes: this can restrain triumphalism, but also flatten confident Christian hope.

## Distortions this module often produces

- It can erode confidence that truth is knowable and sayable.
- It can make every institution seem reducible to domination.
- It can leave people highly critical but poorly formed for constructive fidelity.

## Strengths or truths it often notices

- It exposes false neutrality and hidden power in systems and discourse.
- It warns against triumphalist, flattening narratives.
- It reminds interpreters that they are historically situated and morally accountable.

## Covenantal contrast

The covenantal philosophy receives the warning against prideful certainty and abusive power, yet refuses the collapse of truth into interpretation alone. God speaks truly in history. Language matters, but it does not imprison revelation. Communities can be accountable without dissolving into permanent suspicion.
