# Consumerism

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields

---

## Name

**Module name:** Consumerism

## Description

Consumerism trains people to see the world through preference, acquisition, choice architecture, convenience, and brand-mediated desire. It turns both goods and communities into products for satisfaction.

## Category-by-category summary

### 1. Metaphysics

- Core claim: reality is a marketplace of options organized around desire fulfillment.
- Diagnostic signal: language of fit, customization, and upgrade dominates descriptions of life.
- Notes: creation is experienced as inventory.

### 2. Epistemology

- Core claim: value is known by taste, frictionlessness, and felt satisfaction.
- Diagnostic signal: what is good is what resonates, performs, or serves efficiently.
- Notes: truth is quietly subordinated to user experience.

### 3. Anthropology

- Core claim: humans are choosers, shoppers, and curators of preferred lifestyles.
- Diagnostic signal: church membership, vocation, and spiritual practices are treated as consumable offerings.
- Notes: covenantal obligations feel like poor customer service.

### 4. Ethics

- Core claim: the good is what satisfies desire with minimal cost.
- Diagnostic signal: sacrifice, patience, and long obedience appear irrational or unattractive.
- Notes: discipline becomes an optional premium product.

### 5. Social/Political

- Core claim: communities exist to provide value to members.
- Diagnostic signal: institutions are judged by branding, convenience, and service delivery.
- Notes: loyalty becomes fragile because better options are always imagined to be available.

### 6. Identity/Meaning

- Core claim: meaning is assembled through chosen affiliations, aesthetics, experiences, and brands.
- Diagnostic signal: identity language centers on lifestyle fit and preferred tribe.
- Notes: belonging is consumptive rather than covenantal.

### 7. Eschatology

- Core claim: the future is better choice, less friction, and endless upgrade.
- Diagnostic signal: hope is tethered to access, scale, convenience, and novelty.
- Notes: salvation becomes enhanced experience.

## Distortions this module often produces

- It turns congregations into vendors and members into evaluators.
- It erodes patience with weak, ordinary, or inconvenient neighbors.
- It confuses abundance with blessing and novelty with life.

## Strengths or truths it often notices

- It can reveal when institutions are needlessly opaque or careless about stewardship.
- It reminds leaders that people have real constraints of time, money, and attention.
- It notices the power of beauty, design, and accessibility, though it often misorders them.

## Covenantal contrast

The covenantal philosophy insists that persons are not customers and the people of God are not brands. Goods are received with gratitude, but life is not built by curation. Meaning arises through faithful belonging, patient love, and stewardship under God, not through endless preference satisfaction.
