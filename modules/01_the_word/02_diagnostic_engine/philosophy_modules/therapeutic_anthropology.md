# Therapeutic Anthropology

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields

---

## Name

**Module name:** Therapeutic Anthropology

## Description

Therapeutic anthropology interprets the human person primarily through pain, emotional need, healing, and psychological integration. It often turns pastoral and moral questions into questions of personal wellness.

## Category-by-category summary

### 1. Metaphysics

- Core claim: reality is significant mainly as it affects personal well-being.
- Diagnostic signal: difficult obligations are first interpreted in terms of emotional cost.
- Notes: the world becomes a field of triggers, harms, and healing opportunities.

### 2. Epistemology

- Core claim: inner emotional awareness is a central source of truth.
- Diagnostic signal: statements like "if it feels unsafe, it probably is wrong" settle complex questions.
- Notes: emotions become semi-magisterial rather than important but partial witnesses.

### 3. Anthropology

- Core claim: humans are vulnerable selves in need of validation and healing.
- Diagnostic signal: sin, discipline, and obedience are downplayed in favor of wellness categories.
- Notes: creaturely fragility is real, but therapeutic anthropology often makes fragility the controlling lens.

### 4. Ethics

- Core claim: the good is what preserves emotional health and minimizes distress.
- Diagnostic signal: moral discernment is replaced by the language of boundaries and regulation alone.
- Notes: sacrifice, endurance, and covenantal duty can appear suspicious.

### 5. Social/Political

- Core claim: communities should be organized around emotional safety and affirmation.
- Diagnostic signal: pastoral authority is treated chiefly as a trauma risk.
- Notes: communal life becomes thin because correction and discipline are seen as inherently harmful.

### 6. Identity/Meaning

- Core claim: identity is organized around wounds, recovery, and self-protection.
- Diagnostic signal: personal narratives are dominated by healing vocabulary with little covenant or vocation language.
- Notes: suffering becomes the main interpreter of selfhood.

### 7. Eschatology

- Core claim: the hoped-for future is psychological wholeness and internal peace.
- Diagnostic signal: redemption is described more as inner closure than resurrection or new creation.
- Notes: hope shrinks to wellness.

## Distortions this module often produces

- It elevates emotional comfort over holiness and endurance.
- It weakens moral language by translating nearly everything into healing language.
- It can make communities unable to practice truthful, patient correction.

## Strengths or truths it often notices

- It notices real trauma, exhaustion, and hidden pain that older systems often ignored.
- It insists that people are not simply brains carrying doctrines.
- It reminds ministry contexts that abuse and manipulation do deep damage.

## Covenantal contrast

The covenantal philosophy receives the truth that people are wounded and need care, but refuses to make wound-management the center of anthropology. Human beings are not only sufferers; they are image-bearers, sinners, saints-in-formation, and covenant members called to holiness, love, endurance, and repair.
