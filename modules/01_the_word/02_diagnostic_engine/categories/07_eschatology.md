## Central question

**Where is history going, and what future governs present action?**

## Philosophies that operate strongly in this category

- Progressivist utopianism expects history to bend toward improvement through activism, policy, or enlightened consciousness.
- Consumerism imagines the future as perpetual upgrade.
- Materialism collapses hope into present survival or legacy.
- Therapeutic anthropology imagines the good future as inner peace and personal wholeness.

## Diagnostic questions

- Is hope placed in God’s renewal, institutional reform, technological advance, personal healing, or civilizational progress?
- Does the subject imagine salvation as escape, achievement, optimization, or resurrection?
- Is suffering interpreted only as interruption, or also as a site of faithfulness?
- What future story makes present sacrifices intelligible?

## Covenantal contrast statement

History is moving toward the judgment and renewal of all things in Christ. Christian hope is resurrectional, covenantal, and patient. It empowers faithful labor now without confusing present systems with final redemption.

## Common Western Christian distortions

- Expecting revival, reform, or political success to function as final hope.
- Treating disappointment as proof that God has failed rather than as a summons to steadfastness.
- Replacing resurrection hope with platform growth, institutional momentum, or personal therapeutic arrival.
