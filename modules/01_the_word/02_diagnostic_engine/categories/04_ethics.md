# Ethics

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields

---

## Central question

**What is the good, and how should one live?**

## Philosophies that operate strongly in this category

- Consumerism measures good by satisfaction and preference fulfillment.
- Therapeutic anthropology measures good by emotional safety and felt wholeness.
- Technocratic rationalism measures good by efficiency and outcomes.
- Postmodernism often treats ethics as negotiated power and contextual sensitivity rather than covenant faithfulness.

## Diagnostic questions

- Is goodness framed as holiness, happiness, efficiency, authenticity, harm reduction, or social approval?
- Are commands seen as life-giving boundaries or oppressive restrictions?
- Is sacrifice honored or viewed as a failure of self-care?
- Does the subject assume that what feels healing is therefore morally right?

## Covenantal contrast statement

The good is faithful conformity to God’s character and commands as fulfilled in Christ. Ethics includes love, justice, mercy, truthfulness, holiness, repentance, and covenant loyalty, not merely emotional safety or visible success.

## Common Western Christian distortions

- Replacing obedience language with vague wellness language.
- Reducing holiness to niceness.
- Measuring ministry success more by visible impact than by faithfulness.
