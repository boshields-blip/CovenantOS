# Epistemology

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields

---

## Central question

**How is truth known, trusted, and tested?**

## Philosophies that operate strongly in this category

- Rationalism elevates detached reason as the highest court of appeal.
- Postmodernism emphasizes interpretive instability and suspicion of universal claims.
- Therapeutic anthropology privileges inner feeling as a decisive witness.
- Technocratic rationalism trusts data, process, and expertise as primary knowledge forms.

## Diagnostic questions

- Does the subject appeal first to revelation, tradition, communal wisdom, experience, expertise, or private certainty?
- Is truth treated as something to obey or only something to analyze?
- Are feelings granted authority that should belong to revelation and faithful discernment?
- Is skepticism used as a tool for humility or as a solvent against covenantal confidence?

## Covenantal contrast statement

Truth is known through God’s self-disclosure, received in faith, tested in community, confirmed in obedience, and remembered through Scripture and the apostolic witness. Reason, experience, and expertise are real gifts, but they are not sovereign.

## Common Western Christian distortions

- Treating theology as a purely cerebral system rather than a lived obedience.
- Treating personal authenticity as self-validating truth.
- Treating scholarly sophistication as spiritually superior to ordinary covenant faithfulness.
