# Metaphysics

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields

---

## Central question

**What is ultimately real, and what kind of world are we living in?**

## Philosophies that operate strongly in this category

- Materialism treats matter and measurable processes as ultimate.
- Progressivist utopianism treats history as self-advancing toward improvement.
- Technocratic rationalism treats systems, optimization, and control as the practical structure of reality.
- Postmodernism often destabilizes claims about stable order and givenness.

## Diagnostic questions

- Is reality treated as creation, machine, market, narrative contest, or raw material?
- Is the world received as meaningful gift or handled as neutral stuff?
- Are limits interpreted as creaturely wisdom or as barriers to self-design?
- Does the subject speak as though institutions or systems are more real than persons and covenant bonds?

## Covenantal contrast statement

Reality is created, ordered, relational, and held together in Christ. The world is not self-explaining, spiritually empty, or morally neutral; it is a meaningful creation in which creaturely limits are gifts, not merely constraints.

## Common Western Christian distortions

- Treating the Christian life as mostly "spiritual" while practical life runs on secular assumptions.
- Imagining the church as an abstract entity detached from actual people, meals, vows, and shared life.
- Assuming technology, scale, or efficiency can overcome creaturely limits rather than requiring discernment.
