## Central question

**What is a human being, and what is human life for?**

## Philosophies that operate strongly in this category

- Expressive individualism defines the person as an inward self that must be externally expressed.
- Therapeutic anthropology defines the person as emotionally fragile and in need of affirmation and self-care as highest goods.
- Materialism reduces personhood to biology, desire, or social function.
- Consumerism trains people to imagine themselves as preference-bearing choosers.

## Diagnostic questions

- Is the person understood as received creature, sovereign self, wounded psyche, market chooser, or political unit?
- Are human beings called to communion and stewardship, or mainly to self-discovery and self-protection?
- How are dependence, discipline, and obligation described?
- Does the subject speak of identity as gift or project?

## Covenantal contrast statement

Human beings are image-bearers formed for communion with God, life with neighbor, stewardship in creation, and growth into mature covenant fidelity. They are dignified, fallen, redeemable, and accountable.

## Common Western Christian distortions

- Turning discipleship into self-optimization.
- Treating emotional comfort as the highest pastoral goal.
- Detaching vocation and bodily life from so-called spiritual identity.
