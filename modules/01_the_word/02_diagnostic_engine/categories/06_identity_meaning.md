# Identity and Meaning

## Central question

**Who am I, what gives life meaning, and how is belonging secured?**

## Philosophies that operate strongly in this category

- Expressive individualism treats identity as inward authenticity made public.
- Consumerism treats meaning as curated lifestyle and chosen affinities.
- Therapeutic anthropology treats self-understanding through wounds, healing, and emotional narratives.
- Postmodernism destabilizes inherited identities and privileges self-authored location.

## Diagnostic questions

- Is identity received, negotiated, performed, narrated, or purchased?
- Is belonging covenantal, familial, ecclesial, professional, ideological, or aesthetic?
- What kinds of speech are used: calling, branding, authenticity, platform, impact, journey?
- Does meaning arise from God’s story or from personal fulfillment and recognition?

## Covenantal contrast statement

Identity is received from God within a covenant people and clarified through vocation, holiness, memory, and hope. Meaning is not invented from within the self but discovered in faithful participation in God’s purposes.

## Common Western Christian distortions

- Rebranding discipleship as self-discovery.
- Treating calling mainly as personal fulfillment.
- Equating visibility, platform, or influence with significance.
