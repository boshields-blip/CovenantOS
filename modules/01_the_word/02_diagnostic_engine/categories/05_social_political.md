## Central question

**How should life together be ordered, and where does authority belong?**

## Philosophies that operate strongly in this category

- Liberal individualism imagines society as a collection of rights-bearing autonomous selves.
- Technocratic rationalism privileges expert management and scalable control.
- Progressivist utopianism places hope in historical reform movements and institutions.
- Consumerism reshapes community into networks of services and optional affiliation.

## Diagnostic questions

- Is society imagined as covenant, contract, brand, bureaucracy, or movement?
- Are institutions treated as servants of people or as living agents with their own destiny?
- Is authority personal, accountable, and limited, or abstract, managerial, and disembodied?
- Are local obligations valued, or displaced by platform-scale systems and narratives?

## Covenantal contrast statement

Shared life is ordered under God through accountable relationships, embodied communities, and limited authorities. Family, congregation, neighborhood, vocation, and civil structures are real but not ultimate.

## Common Western Christian distortions

- Talking about "the Church" as though it were a corporate actor apart from actual congregations and saints.
- Outsourcing discipleship to programs, systems, and centralized structures.
- Confusing kingdom faithfulness with partisan strategy or institutional success.
