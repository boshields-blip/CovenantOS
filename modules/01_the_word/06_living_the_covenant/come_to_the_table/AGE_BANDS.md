# Age Bands — Come to the Table

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields

---

## Overview

Come to the Table is designed for the whole household — including those who cannot yet read, those who are just beginning to hold ideas, and those who are ready to engage philosophy. The same passage is heard by all; the host simply opens different doors into it.

The host's primary job in the *Notice* and *Live* movements is to ensure that the youngest band at the table gets the first easy entry, and that the oldest band does not take over.

---

## The six bands

| Band | Rough age | Door in | Expected participation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Little One** | ~0–3 | Heard words; held; sees faces attentive to something | Presence; a word echoed; "Amen" |
| **Image Bearer** | ~3–6 | One concrete image from the passage — a thing, a person, a color, an action | Names the image; repeats one short phrase with the group |
| **Explorer** | ~7–10 | The story beat — who is there, what happens, who God is in it | Retells one moment; asks one question |
| **Inventor** | ~11–14 | Author and audience — who wrote this, to whom, why it might have mattered | Names the author or audience; offers one observation |
| **Apprentice** | ~15+ | Covenantal category — how this passage positions us before God and before one another | Connects the passage to a covenantal frame; leads Berean discussions when appropriate |
| **Mentor** | Any adult or guest | Fellow learner; attentive to what the younger bands are seeing | Draws out younger voices; shares one thing they noticed; never dominates |

Age ranges are approximate. Some ten-year-olds engage as Inventors; some adults need the gentleness of an Image Bearer entry. Use the bands as guides, not gates.

---

## Little One (~0–3)

**What they need:** To hear the words. To see the faces of the people they love turned toward something with care. To feel held while something important is happening.

**What they offer:** Their presence makes the practice physical and honest. They cannot perform. They receive.

**How the host invites them in Notice:**
Name them. *"[Name] is here too."* If they say a word — any word from the passage or not — receive it. *"Yes, light. That's one of the things in the passage."* If they are quiet, that is fine.

**How the host invites them in Live:**
Keep the live invitation concrete and physical. *"This week we're going to name something good God made. [Name] might point at the sky."*

---

## Image Bearer (~3–6)

**What they need:** One vivid image to hold. The passage will have one — a shepherd, a loaf, a star, a voice, a garden, a flame. Name it for them before or just after the reading.

**What they offer:** They often name the image with more precision than expected. They also ask questions that adults have stopped asking. *"Why did He do that?"* is a better question than it sounds like.

**How the host invites them in Notice:**
*"[Name], what's one thing you heard? Something you can see or touch?"* Accept any answer, including silence, including an unrelated response. If they are engaged, follow it with: *"Yes! That's in the passage."*

**How the host invites them in Live:**
Give them the simplest version of the live invitation. If the invitation is "name one good thing God made and thank Him," say: *"[Name], what's something you love that God made?"*

---

## Explorer (~7–10)

**What they need:** The story shape — someone is present, something happens, something changes or is named. The Explorer wants to know *what happened* and *why*.

**What they offer:** They ask the unguarded questions. They notice when the story seems strange or unfair. That noticing is valuable.

**How the host invites them in Notice:**
*"[Name], what's happening in the passage? Who is there?"* Or: *"What did God do here?"* Or: *"What surprised you?"*

**How the host invites them in Live:**
Make the invitation specific and action-shaped. *"Explorer, what is one thing you could do this week that lines up with this?"* Keep the expectation modest — one thing, this week.

---

## Inventor (~11–14)

**What they need:** A question that has a real answer and invites their own thinking. The Inventor wants to be taken seriously as someone who can reason, investigate, and contribute — not just respond.

**What they offer:** They are beginning to hold two ideas at once. They can say "this is like that other passage we read" or "I think Paul is saying X because Y." That connective work is genuinely valuable.

**How the host invites them in Notice:**
*"[Name], do you know who wrote this? Who do you think first heard it?"* Or: *"What do you think the author was trying to say?"*

**How the host invites them in Know:**
If the Know slot this session is *Author* or *Audience*, invite the Inventor to offer what they know before the host fills it in. If they are wrong, that is fine — receive what they offered, then add the session's one point.

**How the host invites them in Live:**
*"Inventor, how would you put the live invitation in your own words?"*

---

## Apprentice (~15+)

**What they need:** To be treated as someone beginning to think philosophically — capable of asking not just "what happened" but "what does this say about reality?" The Apprentice is ready for covenantal categories, authorship patterns, and the beginnings of discernment.

**What they offer:** They can model the full session arc. They can lead a *Notice* round. They can carry the *Know* slot when it reaches the philosophy point. They are the natural leaders of the Berean moments at the table.

**How the host invites them in Notice:**
*"[Name], where does this passage sit in the larger story — what has just happened before this, or what comes after?"* Or: *"What category does this touch — what is it saying about who God is, what a person is, or where history is going?"*

**How the host invites them in Know:**
If the Know slot is *Philosophy*, invite the Apprentice to name the category before the host explains. Encourage them to use the language of the seven The Word categories (metaphysics, epistemology, anthropology, ethics, social/political, identity/meaning, eschatology).

**Berean Tool integration:**
The Apprentice stage is where the Berean Tool enters the table. See [BEREAN_AT_THE_TABLE.md](/the-word/living-the-covenant/come-to-the-table/berean-at-the-table) for the full treatment, including guardrails, sample script, and how to adapt the tool to a household.

The Apprentice learns at this stage that loving critique of teaching is not a contradiction of charity — it is a form of it. They learn to ask "what is true here?" before "what is off here?" and to end every conversation with "what can we receive from this?"

---

## Mentor (any adult or guest)

**What they need:** Permission to learn, not to teach. The Mentor role is defined not by knowledge but by posture — an adult who brings curiosity, who models charitable observation, who asks questions rather than closing them down.

**What they offer:** Experience, patience, and the credibility that comes from having walked with Scripture for years. When a Mentor says "I didn't see that before," it gives every younger band permission to keep learning.

**How the host invites them in Notice:**
Save the Mentor's turn for later in the round. *"[Mentor name], what did you notice?"* Keep their contribution brief.

**How the host invites them in Live:**
*"[Mentor name], how have you seen this lived out?"* One story, one image. Then close.

**The Mentor's non-negotiable:** The Mentor does not dominate. If they begin to lecture, the host gently names the transition. This is a protection for the younger bands, and it is also a gift to the Mentor.
