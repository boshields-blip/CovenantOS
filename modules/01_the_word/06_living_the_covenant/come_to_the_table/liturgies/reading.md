# Reading — Come to the Table

## Purpose

This document describes how Scripture is read during movement 3 (*Read*) of a Come to the Table session.

---

## Read from a real Bible

Whenever possible, read from a physical Bible. This is not a rule against digital access; it is a practice of formation. A physical book has a history, a weight, a presence. Children who grow up with a real Bible on the table learn, in a bodily way, that these words come from somewhere and belong to something larger than a screen.

Open the Bible to the passage. Read it from the page. If the Bible has marginal notes, you may glance at them in preparation, but do not read them at the table.

---

## Read slowly

Most people read aloud faster than the words can land. The passage is short — usually three to ten verses. There is no reason to rush. Read one sentence. Pause. Read the next. Let the words have room.

Reading slowly also signals to younger children that this is not a quick announcement. The change of pace communicates that something different is happening.

---

## The youngest hears it twice

When Little Ones or Image Bearers are present, read the passage twice. Not twice in a row with explanation between — simply twice, at the same pace.

Young children do not process text the way older children do. The first reading familiarizes them with the sounds and the shape. The second reading is often when a single image or word catches their attention.

---

## Older children may read

Invite an Explorer, Inventor, or Apprentice to read aloud when appropriate. This is not a test of reading ability — it is an invitation to participate in an act that the community values.

If an older child reads, give a short preparation: *"Read slowly. Pause at the commas. If you're not sure how to say a word, just say it the way you think it sounds."*

When the older child finishes, say thank you. That is all.

---

## Do not explain as you read

Read the passage first. Talk about it during *Notice*.

If you pause to explain a word or a reference as you read, the passage never has a chance to speak for itself. Younger children especially need to hear the whole passage as a unit before they can begin to notice anything within it.

If there is a word that is clearly unfamiliar — a name, a place, a Hebrew or Greek term — you may say it naturally and continue. Do not stop to define it. The *Know* slot is where language notes belong.

---

## After the reading

When you have finished reading (the second time, if applicable), place the Bible on the table where it is visible. Let a moment pass.

Then move to *Notice*: *"What did you hear?"*
