# The Re-formation Pathway

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields | **Last updated:** 2026-08-01

---

## Why this document exists

Seeing is not the goal of The Word. Re-formation is.

The movements before this one exist to make something possible: to root you in the world of the text ([The Land of the Text](/the-word/the-land-of-the-text)), teach you its language ([The Language of the Land](/the-word/the-language-of-the-land)), let you walk its passages ([Walking the Text](/the-word/walking-the-text)), hold up a mirror to your own convictions ([Knowing Your Own Way](/the-word/knowing-your-own-way)), and help you weigh the streams that formed you ([The Streams of the Traditions](/the-word/the-streams-of-the-traditions)). But seeing clearly is only the beginning. Sight that never becomes a re-formed life is just a more sophisticated way of knowing — and knowledge without renewal hardens into pride.

The Re-formation Pathway is the hinge. It takes everything the earlier movements reveal and turns it toward repentance, renewal, and embodied practice. It answers the practical question the rest of The Word leaves open: **now that I can see, how do I actually walk a different way?**

---

## The shape of biblical re-formation

The New Testament does not describe change as self-improvement, optimization, or the accumulation of better ideas. It describes it as a threefold movement: *put off* the old, *be renewed* in the mind, *put on* the new (Ephesians 4:22–24; Colossians 3:9–10; Romans 12:2). This is not a technique. It is the ordinary grammar of dying and rising with Christ, worked out in the concrete details of thought, speech, habit, and shared life.

The pathway keeps that grammar in three movements, each reaching forward into an embodied practice.

```text
Put off  →  Renew the mind  →  Put on
  ↑                                 |
  |_________ practiced in __________|
        Come to the Table
        Harvest & Hymn
```

The loop is not linear and finished. It is a returning cycle, walked again in each season and stage of life. In Hebraic terms this is *teshuvah* — turning, returning — not a single decision but a direction of life.

## Movement 1 — Put off

**Put off the old reading, the old assumptions, the old speech, and the old habits.**

Putting off begins where the earlier movements led. Having walked the text in its own world, and having traced your own way through the seven questions in [Knowing Your Own Way](/the-word/knowing-your-own-way), you can now name specific things that have bent your imagination away from the grain of the text. This is not a vague resolution to "do better." It is particular.

The most basic thing to put off is a way of *reading* — the modern habit of coming to Scripture as a tourist rather than a native: lifting verses out of their world, dropping ancient words into a modern frame, reading a covenant book as though it addressed an autonomous individual. Setting that down is the first act of re-formation, because how you read shapes everything you become.

Beneath the reading are the assumptions that drive it — the [cultural currents](/the-word/knowing-your-own-way/the-cultural-currents) that shape nearly everyone: consumerism, expressive individualism, technocratic rationalism. Putting off asks, honestly:

- Where do I still read and live as though the world were neutral stuff to manage rather than a creation to receive?
- Where has my sense of self been formed more by expressive individualism than by the image-bearer I actually am?
- Where has faithfulness been quietly replaced by safety, efficiency, or being seen?
- Where have I let a borrowed vocabulary — brand, platform, my truth, my peace — train my imagination without my noticing?

Putting off is confession before it is correction. It names what must be set down without pretending the person who carried it was a villain — including when that person is you. The posture is the one the whole module requires: truthful, gentle, unhurried. Repentance, not self-contempt. The aim is to set something down, not to flog yourself for having carried it.

A caution belongs here. Putting off is not the same as throwing away every good thing your tradition gave you. As [The Streams of the Traditions](/the-word/the-streams-of-the-traditions) insists, every stream carries real gifts alongside its blind spots. Re-formation keeps the gift and sets down the distortion. It does not burn the house to kill the moth.

## Movement 2 — Renew the mind

**Return to the world of the text, its language, its story, and a rightly ordered imagination.**

Putting something down leaves a space. If that space is not filled, the old pattern returns — often in a subtler form. Renewal is the deliberate re-furnishing of the imagination, and it draws directly on the first movements of The Word:

- **Return to the land of the text.** For each place you named a drift, go back to the world the text assumes and let its answer reshape you — not as information to file, but as a truth to be re-formed by. Ask: *what would it mean to actually see this way?* (See [The Land of the Text](/the-word/the-land-of-the-text).)
- **Retrain speech.** Because language is the soil imagination grows in, renewal is partly a matter of words. Learning to think in *dabar*, *hesed*, and *shalom* — and noticing when you reach for "build the brand" or "protect my peace" instead — is not cosmetic. Changed speech is catechesis. (See [The Language of the Land](/the-word/the-language-of-the-land).)
- **Recover memory.** Covenant renewal is historical. It works through Scripture read aloud, the testimony of the saints, and liturgy that carries a community's memory forward. The mind is renewed less by novelty than by re-inhabiting what was given — the story into which you were baptized.
- **Re-order the imagination Hebraically.** Renewal moves thought back toward the concrete: bodies, tables, seasons, places, obligations, and names — rather than abstractions floating free of any actual household.

Renewal is not a single insight. It is repeated return. The mind is renewed the way a field is kept — not once, but every season.

---

## Movement 3 — Put on

**Practice covenantal habits, truthful language, embodied obedience, and communal faithfulness.**

Renewal that never becomes practice remains an idea, and ideas do not form people. Character is formed by what is repeated, embodied, and shared. This is why the pathway does not end in the mind. It ends at a table and in a season.

Putting on is the move from *seeing rightly* to *living covenantally*, and it is inescapably communal. You cannot put on covenant identity alone, because covenant is by definition a bond between persons. This is where The Word hands formation over to its practices — and, further downstream, to the household and the community.

Putting on happens through:

- **Covenant habits.** Small, repeated, embodied acts — daily Scripture, honest confession, hospitality, keeping promises, showing up — that train the affections over time. Formation is cumulative: the long obedience that consumerism finds unattractive and covenant finds ordinary.
- **Truthful language in practice.** Not merely knowing the better phrase, but speaking it in real conversation and prayer until it becomes native.
- **The two anchoring practices:**
  - [**Come to the Table**](/the-word/living-the-covenant/come-to-the-table) — the weekly household practice where provision becomes formation: a passage read aloud, heard, discussed, and prayed, at an actual table, across the whole range of ages. This is where "put on" happens week by week.
  - [**Harvest & Hymn**](/the-word/living-the-covenant/harvest-and-hymn) — the quarterly, seasonal community gathering where households bring shared meal, creative offering, and testimony. Where formation moves outward from the household table into the wider community.

Putting on is the point where The Word stops being a body of content and becomes a way of life.

---

## The loop returns

The three movements are not a staircase climbed once. They are a cycle, walked again and again:

1. **Put off** — set down the old reading and the borrowed assumptions (seeing becomes confession)
2. **Return** — renew the imagination from the world of the text (truth re-furnishes the space)
3. **Walk in the way** — practice the covenant life (renewal becomes habit and shared life)

Each turn goes deeper. A drift you named this year will look different when you return to it after a season of practice. The self-examination in [Knowing Your Own Way](/the-word/knowing-your-own-way) is meant to be repeated, not completed. Re-formation is *teshuvah* as a settled direction — a life that keeps turning back toward covenant — not a problem solved and closed.

---

## Where this pathway leads

The Re-formation Pathway is the last movement of The Word, but not the end of the formation it begins. A covenant imagination does not stay in the head; it reorders a home. The person formed at the table begins to see labor, provision, land, and hospitality differently — the work of **[The Household](/the-household)**. And rightly ordered households, reaching toward one another, begin to form the shared public life that is the work of **[The Compact](/the-compact)**.

```text
The Word → forms imagination
    ↓
The Household → households form communities
    ↓
The Compact
```

The re-formed imagination is the seed. The household is the soil. The community is the harvest. This pathway is where the seed goes into the ground.

---

## A closing posture

Everything in The Word has aimed at this: not clever insight, not a refined ability to spot what is wrong in others, but a re-formed people who see, speak, and live covenantally. Seeing that does not become re-formation curdles into pride. Re-formation that does not begin in confession collapses into performance.

The way through is the oldest way there is. Put off. Be renewed. Put on. Turn, and keep turning. And do it not alone, but at a table, in a season, among a people, under God.
