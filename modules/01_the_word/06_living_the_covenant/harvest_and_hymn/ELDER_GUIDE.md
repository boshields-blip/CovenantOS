# Elder Guide — Harvest & Hymn

---

## The theology under this role

Peter charges elders to shepherd the flock allotted to them, "not domineering over those in your charge, but being examples to the flock" (1 Peter 5:2–3) — the same word, *kleros*, that [The Sacred and the Secular](/the-word/walking-the-text/the-sacred-and-the-secular) traces back to its root meaning: an inheritance held in trust, never a rank to be occupied. Ephesians 4 says the same thing from the other side: those given to teach and equip are given "to equip the saints for the work of ministry" (Ephesians 4:12), not to do the ministry for them.

An elder at Harvest & Hymn stands under both of those charges. The role is real. It is not a formality, and it is not interchangeable with any other seat at the table. But it exists to multiply the priesthood of the whole gathering, not to replace it — and an elder who has quietly become the center of every gathering has stopped doing the job Peter and Paul actually describe, however good his intentions.

---

## What the elder offers

At each gathering, the elder brings three things, held loosely and offered briefly:

1. A concise seasonal teaching
2. A practical household practice to carry into the quarter
3. A closing blessing for sending

---

## Seasonal teaching examples

- **Spring:** "Sow righteousness; reap steadfast love."
- **Summer:** "Abide in the Vine."
- **Fall:** "He gives seed to the sower and bread to the eater."
- **Winter:** "In returning and rest you shall be saved."

These are teaching seeds, not sermons. A household should be able to remember and carry one of these into an ordinary Tuesday; that is a better measure of success than how much ground the teaching covered.

---

## What sacrificial, non-dominating leadership looks like here

Authority that serves rather than dominates is not a vague ideal in Scripture. It has a shape, and that shape is visible in small, concrete choices:

- Keep the equipping moment inside its agreed time. An elder who runs long is teaching the gathering, without meaning to, that his voice matters more than the household's.
- Name and affirm what families and children brought, specifically and by name, before adding anything of your own.
- Invite the quiet households without pressure. Some families need to be asked twice before they believe the invitation is real.
- Resist turning the gathering into a lecture hall. If you notice yourself wanting to say one more thing, that is usually the moment to stop.
- End with blessing, not extended commentary. The last word belongs to God's kindness toward the gathering, not to the elder's final point.

This is the same authority [The Ordered Household](/the-household/the-ordered-household) describes at the kitchen table and [The Two and the One](/the-word/walking-the-text/the-two-and-the-one) describes in marriage: leadership that lays itself down for the people it serves, never leadership that manages them.
