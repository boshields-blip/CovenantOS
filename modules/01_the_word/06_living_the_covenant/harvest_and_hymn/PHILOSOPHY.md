# Philosophy — Harvest & Hymn

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields

---

## Anchoring text

Harvest & Hymn is anchored in **1 Corinthians 14:26**:

> "When you come together, each one has a hymn, a lesson, a revelation, a tongue, or an interpretation. Let all things be done for building up."

The quarterly gathering is not a performance by a few, but a covenantal assembly where each household brings something for the strengthening of all.

---

## Agricultural and biblical rhythm

Harvest & Hymn follows the four-season cadence because creation itself witnesses to ordered time (Genesis 8:22). Planting, tending, harvesting, and resting are not merely farm tasks; they are formational metaphors embedded in creaturely life.

Biblical festival life likewise gathered households in recurring rhythms of remembrance, thanksgiving, and covenant renewal. Harvest & Hymn sits in that stream: regular communal gatherings that reinforce gratitude, shared life, and embodied memory.

---

## Table-centered liturgy

The table is central because covenant life is embodied. Families bring real food, real stories, and real offerings from the season. The meal is not an accessory to the gathering; it is one of the liturgical anchors.

---

## Elder leadership that equips

The elder's role is to equip and bless, not dominate the gathering. Teaching is concise, seasonally grounded, and aimed at household practice. The elder strengthens participation rather than replacing it.

---

## Every family brings something

A healthy gathering expects contribution from each household: a dish, a testimony, a song, a poem, a reading, a prayer, or another fitting offering. This keeps the gathering communal, not consumer-driven.

Children are not an afterthought. They bring and participate fully in creative offerings, meal reflection, and shared blessing.
