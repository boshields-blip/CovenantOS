# Workshops — Harvest & Hymn

---

The five movements in [Gathering Format](/the-word/living-the-covenant/harvest-and-hymn/gathering-format) are the whole liturgy, and a gathering that only ever does those five things has lost nothing. Workshops exist for households who want to go a step further: hands-on, seasonal skill-sharing, offered before or after the canonical gathering, that lets one household's competence become a gift to the others.

This is subsidiarity at the scale of a single afternoon. Nobody needs to hire a class to learn to start seed or bake bread; somebody in the gathering probably already knows how, and a workshop is simply the structure that lets them teach it. Workshops are optional and supplementary. They should never replace or crowd out the five liturgical movements themselves.

## Spring

- Seed starting
- Composting
- Psalm-writing for kids

## Summer

- Herbal medicine
- Songwriting
- Garden tours

## Fall

- Canning
- Bread baking
- Harvest psalm night

## Winter

- Mending and crafting
- Storytelling
- Quiet worship nights

A workshop does not need an instructor with credentials. It needs one household willing to say, "we know how to do this, come learn with us" — which is itself a small enactment of everything [Subsidiarity](/the-compact/subsidiarity) and [Peoplehood](/the-compact/peoplehood) describe on a larger scale.
