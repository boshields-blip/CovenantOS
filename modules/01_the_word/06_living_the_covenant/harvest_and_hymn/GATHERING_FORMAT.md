# Gathering Format — Harvest & Hymn

---

## The canonical quarterly shape

Every Harvest & Hymn gathering follows the same five movements, the same way every Come to the Table session follows the same seven. The structure stays fixed on purpose — a family should never have to relearn how the gathering works — while the content inside each movement changes with the season. See [Seasonal Rhythm](/the-word/living-the-covenant/harvest-and-hymn/seasonal-rhythm) for what fills each movement quarter to quarter.

**Total target time: ~1 hour 45 minutes to 2 hours 30 minutes**, including the meal.

---

## The five movements

| # | Movement | Target time | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Arrival and table setting** | 15–20 min | Families arrive, place the food they brought, and help set the shared table. A short seasonal blessing marks the gathering's start. |
| 2 | **Shared meal** | 45–60 min | Households share what they brought and name one thing the season has taught them. This movement is nourishment and testimony at once. |
| 3 | **Creative offerings** | 30–45 min | Families and children bring what the season stirred in them: a psalm, a poem, a song, a chant, a drawing, a testimony, a reading, a prayer. |
| 4 | **The elder's equipping moment** | 10–15 min | The elder gives a brief seasonal teaching, names one concrete household practice for the coming quarter, and blesses the gathering. See [Elder Guide](/the-word/living-the-covenant/harvest-and-hymn/elder-guide). |
| 5 | **Closing blessing and sending** | ~5 min | A short seasonal sending concludes the gathering and releases households back into weekly table life. |

---

## Notes on each movement

### 1. Arrival and table setting

This movement is not dead time before the "real" gathering starts. Helping set a shared table, together, is itself an act of covenant participation — every household has a hand in making the meal possible before anyone sits down to eat it.

### 2. Shared meal

The meal is not an intermission. As households share what they brought, invite one sentence from each about what the season has actually taught them — not a polished testimony, just an honest word.

### 3. Creative offerings

This is 1 Corinthians 14:26 in practice: each household brings something, so that no one is only an audience. Offerings do not need to be accomplished. A four-year-old's drawing counts as much as a grandfather's psalm.

### 4. The elder's equipping moment

Short on purpose. The elder is equipping the gathering, not replacing what each household has already brought. See [Elder Guide](/the-word/living-the-covenant/harvest-and-hymn/elder-guide) for the theology behind this movement's shape.

### 5. Closing blessing and sending

The sending is brief but real: it marks the end of the quarterly gathering and hands households back to the weekly rhythm of [Come to the Table](/the-word/living-the-covenant/come-to-the-table) until the next season turns.
