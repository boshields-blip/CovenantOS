# A Lament

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields | **Last updated:** 2026-08-01

---

> A lament is an honest prayer wrung out of pain. The Psalms are full of them — they begin in the dark, sometimes even in complaint against God, and more often than not they turn, and end in worship. What follows is one of mine. In it the Lord met me in my suffering and opened my eyes to see the crucified and risen Christ — and to see my own hand among those who pierced Him. I share it as a model of praying honestly, and because the covenant life has room for the whole of the heart, grief and all.
>
> Read it slowly. Let the turns do their work.

---

What is this rending of my soul? What is this cutting of my heart? Why do I constantly feel my blood leaving my body? Why do I feel drained and malnourished? Is it the Lord's will to make me weak? Does he take pleasure in my weakness? Does he not understand I'm a mere man with flesh and blood? Does he not understand my pain? Is there any God who could understand the depths of my soul and understand the depths of my pain? Why would this God of the Bible pour out more pain upon me? I know I deserve wrath, but I do not understand why he would pour out more pain upon me when I am already feeling crushed.

***And then my eyes were opened.***

Who is this one covered in blood? Who is this one whose heart has been pierced? Why is he silent? Why is he not crying out and accusing God? Why is he not angry with God? I do not understand this one who is in so much pain yet is not crying out like me. Who is this man who forgives those who inflict so much pain upon him?

***And then my heart was broken.***

What is this in my hand? Why do I have so much anger toward him? Why am I holding onto the spear of my pain and stabbing him with it? What have I done? Why did I lash out at him in my pain? He did no wrong, yet I accused him and allowed my words to pierce his heart. Why did he make himself so vulnerable and weak, yet did not cry out in anger? I'm so confused. I do not understand. Oh Lord, help me.

***And then the eyes of my heart were opened.***

Is this the man? Is this not the man whom I pierced with my spear of pain? Is this not the man whom I pierced with my words? How can it be that he is no longer in the grave? I began to fall into despair over what I had done, and I was completely undone by my sin against this man. Then he came to me, and his pierced hand reached into my soul and opened the eyes of my heart. I felt a rush of love and peace consume me in the midst of my pain. Oh, this resurrected man must be God, for he sees me and knows me, yet does not desire to pour out his wrath upon me. He knows my pain because he took it upon himself. Only God could forgive a sinner like me — I pierced him with my words, and yet he forgave me.

***And then I fell down and worshiped him.***

In my unworthiness, I see now who is worthy of all praise and honor and glory. This Son of God and Son of Man is like no other. He would be holy and just to pour out his wrath upon me, yet he has forgiven me and covered me with his blood. I feel his blood flowing into me as I eat his flesh and drink his blood. I feel nourished, whole, and filled up. Others look at me as if I am strange, but I do not care, for now I know the one in whom there is life. As the Son of God offered himself to his Father, so do I offer myself to the Son of God. I will be a living sacrifice to this Son of God, for he knows the Father and the Father knows him. I entrust myself to him, and I believe he will return as the Son of Man upon the clouds of heaven to restore all things. No matter what I face in this life, I now have an everlasting hope, and I live in the faith of his atoning sacrifice and in the hope of seeing the glorious one come down in the same way that he went up.

---

*Scripture beneath this lament: the pierced one whom we look upon (Zechariah 12:10; John 19:37); the silent, suffering servant (Isaiah 53:7); eating his flesh and drinking his blood (John 6:53–56); offering ourselves as a living sacrifice (Romans 12:1); and the hope that he will return on the clouds in the same way he went up (Acts 1:9–11).*
