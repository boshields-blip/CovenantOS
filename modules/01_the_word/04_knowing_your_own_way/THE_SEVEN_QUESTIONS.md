# The Seven Questions

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields | **Last updated:** 2026-08-01

---

Seven questions run underneath every life, whether or not we ever say them out loud. Your answers to them are your *way* — the path your imagination actually walks. Here they are asked of your own heart, one at a time. For each: the question, why it matters, some honest things to ask yourself, and the answer that arises from the land of the text — the plumb line, not a slogan.

Take them slowly. One at a sitting is plenty. Where a question stings or catches, stay with it rather than moving on.

---

## 1. What is real? *(Metaphysics)*

**Why it matters:** What you treat as most real shapes everything else — how you spend your hours, what you trust, what you fear.

**Ask yourself:** When I am not thinking about it, what do I actually treat as the most real thing — money, matter, systems, my own experience, God? Do I receive the world as a gift I am given, or as neutral stuff I manage? Are limits an insult to overcome, or a wisdom to live within?

**What arises from the land of the text:** Reality is created, ordered, and held together by a God who speaks and sustains it. The world is not empty matter or a machine; it is a meaningful creation, and its limits are gifts. To be a creature is not a problem to solve but a truth to inhabit.

---

## 2. How do I know what is true? *(Epistemology)*

**Why it matters:** Where you go first for truth reveals who your real authority is.

**Ask yourself:** When I need to know what is true, what do I appeal to first — my feelings, expert consensus, my private reasoning, my tradition, Scripture? Is truth something I mainly analyze, or something I am willing to obey? Do I let my emotions carry an authority that belongs to God's word and to faithful discernment?

**What arises from the land of the text:** Truth is known through God's self-disclosure, received in trust, tested in community, and confirmed in obedience. Reason, experience, and expertise are real gifts, but none of them is sovereign. Knowing is not merely gathering information; it is rightly ordered attention before God.

---

## 3. What is a person for? *(Anthropology)*

**Why it matters:** What you think a human being *is* determines what you think a good life *does*.

**Ask yourself:** Do I see myself mainly as a sovereign chooser, a wounded psyche to be soothed, a consumer of experiences, or an image-bearer made for communion and stewardship? Do I speak of my identity as something I discover and receive, or something I construct and perform? How do I talk about dependence, discipline, and obligation — as burdens, or as the shape of a real life?

**What arises from the land of the text:** A human being is an image-bearer, formed for communion with God, life with neighbor, and stewardship of creation, growing into mature covenant faithfulness. Dignified, fallen, redeemable, accountable — a creature, not an autonomous self, and not merely a bundle of needs.

---

## 4. What is the good? *(Ethics)*

**Why it matters:** Everyone lives toward some vision of "the good." Naming yours shows you what you are actually serving.

**Ask yourself:** When I call something good, do I mean holy, or happy, or efficient, or authentic, or safe, or approved of? Do I experience God's commands as life-giving boundaries or as restrictions to negotiate? Is sacrifice something I honor, or something I treat as a failure of self-care? Do I assume that whatever feels healing must therefore be right?

**What arises from the land of the text:** The good is faithfulness to God's character and commands, fulfilled in Christ — love, justice, mercy, truthfulness, holiness, repentance, and covenant loyalty. Not merely emotional safety or visible success, and not self-expression, but a life bent to the grain of how God made the world.

---

## 5. How should we live together? *(Social & Political)*

**Why it matters:** Your imagination of community determines whether you can actually belong to one.

**Ask yourself:** Do I imagine the people around me as a covenant, a contract, a brand I chose, a bureaucracy, or a movement? Do I treat institutions as servants of people, or as living things with their own destiny? Is the authority I recognize personal and accountable, or abstract and managerial? Do I value the obligations of my actual place, or have I traded them for platform-scale belonging?

**What arises from the land of the text:** Shared life is ordered under God through accountable relationships, embodied communities, and limited authorities — household, congregation, neighborhood, vocation. These are real and honorable, but none of them is ultimate. Covenant, not contract, is the deep grammar of life together.

---

## 6. Who am I? *(Identity & Meaning)*

**Why it matters:** Where you locate the "real you" determines what you will sacrifice everything else to protect.

**Ask yourself:** Where do I locate my truest self — in my choices, my story, my wounds, my work, my calling? Is my belonging covenantal and given, or curated and chosen? What words do I reach for — authenticity, brand, platform, journey, calling, inheritance? Does my sense of meaning come from God's story, or from being seen and fulfilled?

**What arises from the land of the text:** Identity is received before it is performed — given by God, within a covenant people, clarified through vocation, holiness, memory, and hope. Meaning is not invented from within the self but discovered by taking your place in God's story. You are named before you achieve anything.

---

## 7. Where is it all going? *(Eschatology)*

**Why it matters:** The future you actually believe in is what makes your present sacrifices make sense — or not.

**Ask yourself:** Where do I really place my hope — in God's renewal, in political or institutional wins, in technological progress, in personal healing, in my legacy? Do I imagine salvation as escape, as achievement, as optimization, or as resurrection? When I suffer or am disappointed, do I read it only as interruption, or also as a place where faithfulness is forged?

**What arises from the land of the text:** History is moving toward the judgment and renewal of all things in Christ — not escape from the world but the healing of it. Christian hope is resurrectional, covenantal, and patient. It frees you to labor faithfully now without confusing any present system, victory, or breakthrough with the final thing.

---

## After the mirror

You will not answer these once and be done. The point is to return — a question a season, across years — and watch your answers change as your life is reshaped by the world of the text. Where you found your instinct running against the grain, do not despair and do not merely resolve to try harder. Notice where the instinct came from (the next page, [The Cultural Currents](/the-word/knowing-your-own-way/the-cultural-currents), and [The Streams of the Traditions](/the-word/the-streams-of-the-traditions), will help), and carry it into [Living the Covenant](/the-word/living-the-covenant), where seeing becomes practice.
