# Sample Berean Profile: Composite Illustrative Figure

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields

---

> **Note on this document:** This is a worked example using a clearly fictional, composite "type." It does not represent any real person. It is designed to show what a complete, charitable profile can look like when the Berean Tool is used well.

---

## Subject overview

- **Name:** A Composite Evangelical Pastor (illustrative, fictional)
- **Tradition:** American evangelical, broadly Reformed in doctrine, with revivalist and church-growth influences
- **Education / lineage:** College at a broadly evangelical Christian liberal arts institution; seminary at a doctrinally conservative evangelical school with a practical ministry emphasis
- **Key corpus references:** Expository sermon series, books on leadership and discipleship, podcast on living the Christian life
- **High-level diagnostic summary:** This composite pastor reflects the best instincts of the evangelical tradition — biblical seriousness, genuine mission-heartedness, and care for real people. At the same time, the inherited vocabulary of authenticity, impact, personal calling, and practical success can subtly bend the theology toward expressive individualism, pragmatism, and therapeutic anthropology.

---

## Strengths to honor

Before any critical analysis, pause here. These strengths are real, not rhetorical concessions. They represent genuine goods that the tradition has preserved and this type of teacher often models well.

- **Biblical fidelity:** This tradition prizes the authority of Scripture and transmits genuine love for the text.
- **Evangelistic urgency:** The revivalist inheritance produces a real care for whether people know and trust Jesus — not merely belong to a system.
- **Accessibility:** This stream worked hard to make theological content understandable to ordinary people. That is a significant act of service.
- **Missional zeal:** There is genuine sacrifice behind the pragmatic emphases — a conviction that mission matters and that complacency is unfaithful.
- **Care for individuals:** Therapeutic language, when used well, emerges from real attentiveness to real people in real pain. That attentiveness is a virtue.

---

## Identified influences

- **Primary influences:** Baptist revivalism, evangelical pragmatism, Reformed soteriology, American church-growth movement
- **Secondary influences:** Therapeutic anthropology (cultural absorption rather than intentional adoption), expressive individualism (mostly in identity and formation language)
- **Notable tensions or mixed inheritances:** The formal theology is theocentric and Reformed, but the operational vocabulary for discipleship, ministry, and personal growth often carries expressive and therapeutic assumptions.

---

## Seven-category analysis

### 1. Metaphysics

- **Observed pattern:** Creation and everyday life are treated as meaningful primarily when they provide opportunities for conversion, spiritual transformation, or missional engagement.
- **Likely influences:** Revivalist prioritization of spiritual over material, Protestant suspicion of ordinary creaturely life as a site of formation.
- **Strengths observed:** The tradition correctly sees that the world has a purpose beyond itself, that history matters, and that ordinary life is not ultimate.
- **Common Western drift callout:** A practical dualism can emerge where "spiritual" work carries dignity and "ordinary" work (plumbing, farming, cooking, care) is quietly second-class.
- **Covenantal contrast:** Creation is not merely a stage for evangelism; it is itself a theater of God's glory and a site of covenantal stewardship. Ordinary work carries genuine dignity.

### 2. Epistemology

- **Observed pattern:** Truth is known primarily through Scripture interpreted by trained pastors, supplemented by personal spiritual experience and practical fruit.
- **Likely influences:** Protestant sola scriptura tradition (positive), evangelical individualism in interpretation, pragmatic test of effectiveness.
- **Strengths observed:** The commitment to Scripture as authority is a genuine and costly inheritance. Many teachers in this tradition have resisted cultural pressure precisely because of this anchoring.
- **Common Western drift callout:** "Does it work?" and "Does it move me?" can quietly function as supplementary authorities, sometimes outranking exegetical conclusions that are less emotionally resonant or immediately useful.
- **Covenantal contrast:** Truth is known through Scripture read in community, tested in obedience, and received with historical memory. The community of interpreters through time is a genuine epistemic gift.

### 3. Anthropology

- **Observed pattern:** Human beings are primarily understood as spiritual seekers who need to be converted, then helped toward personal growth and discipleship. Identity is often framed around one's story, calling, and healing.
- **Likely influences:** Revivalist emphasis on individual decision, therapeutic culture (absorbed), expressive individualism (absorbed).
- **Strengths observed:** The emphasis on the dignity of every person before God, and the genuine care for individual souls, is a real strength of this stream.
- **Common Western drift callout:** "Personal relationship with God" can become functionally individualistic, with the community of the church becoming a support structure for private spiritual development.
- **Covenantal contrast:** Human beings are image-bearers called into communion, not just individual seekers who happen to gather. Formation is communal, embodied, and historically situated — not purely inward.

### 4. Ethics

- **Observed pattern:** Ethical life is framed around personal holiness, avoiding obvious sin, and maintaining integrity in one's private walk. Ministry ethics often adds effectiveness, faithfulness, and witness.
- **Likely influences:** Pietist moral seriousness, evangelical personal holiness tradition, pragmatic ministry culture.
- **Strengths observed:** The seriousness about personal integrity and the refusal to separate doctrinal belief from practical life are genuine contributions of this stream.
- **Common Western drift callout:** Holiness can narrow to personal moral management, disconnected from corporate justice, neighbor-love, creation care, or the political and social dimensions of covenant faithfulness.
- **Covenantal contrast:** Goodness is covenant fidelity — which includes personal holiness but is not exhausted by it. Love, justice, mercy, truthfulness, and neighbor-care are equally covenantal concerns.

### 5. Social/Political

- **Observed pattern:** The church is primarily a voluntary gathering of believers for worship, teaching, and mutual encouragement. Institutional life is justified primarily by its function in mission.
- **Likely influences:** American voluntarism, church-growth organizational theory, Protestant ecclesiology emphasizing gathered believers.
- **Strengths observed:** The genuine warmth and relational hospitality of many evangelical congregations is not fake — it is a real fruit of this tradition's care for individuals.
- **Common Western drift callout:** The church can quietly become a vendor of spiritual services rather than a covenant community. Attendance becomes participation, and programs become substitutes for shared life.
- **Covenantal contrast:** The church is a covenant people — not an organization, not a service provider, not a movement. Membership implies obligation, discipline, shared life, and mutual accountability.

### 6. Identity/Meaning

- **Observed pattern:** Identity is primarily narrated as "my story with God" — conversion, growth, calling, and purpose. Platform, impact, and influence are often treated as signs of significance.
- **Likely influences:** Revivalist conversion narrative, expressive individualism, evangelical leadership culture.
- **Strengths observed:** The genuine care for each person having a real, first-person faith — not just inherited religion — reflects something the Reformation rightly recovered. That instinct is not the problem.
- **Common Western drift callout:** "My story" can subtly displace "God's story into which I have been written." Platform, visibility, and recognized impact can quietly become the functional measures of meaning.
- **Covenantal contrast:** Identity is received within God's covenant people and clarified through faithful vocation, holy obedience, and participation in the communion of saints — not constructed through visibility or inner authenticity alone.

### 7. Eschatology

- **Observed pattern:** Hope is primarily located in personal salvation (being with God after death) and in missional impact (helping others know Jesus before the end). Revival and spiritual awakening are often treated as the great historical hope.
- **Likely influences:** Pietist other-worldliness, revivalist urgency, American optimism about spiritual possibility.
- **Strengths observed:** The urgency about eternity and the refusal to treat present circumstances as ultimate represent a genuine eschatological seriousness that the broader culture has lost.
- **Common Western drift callout:** Eschatology can collapse into either escapism (just wait to get to heaven) or revivalist utopianism (the next awakening will solve everything). The present body, the local place, and ordinary creation can be thinned out.
- **Covenantal contrast:** Christian hope is resurrectional and creational — not escape, not merely personal survival. The whole creation is being moved toward renewal. Present obedience, embodied fidelity, and creaturely stewardship matter because of that future.

## Formation considerations

These are not indictments. They are invitations arising from the analysis above.

- **Recover the ordinary:** Re-learn that plumbing, farming, parenting, and neighborhood care are genuinely holy callings, not staging areas for evangelism.
- **Thicken community:** Ask what disciplines and structures move a congregation from a gathering of individuals who share a preference toward a covenant people who share a life.
- **Reground identity:** Practice speaking more often of "the story God is writing" and less often of "my story" — small but formative shift.
- **Expand ethical imagination:** Add justice, creation care, and neighbor-love to the moral vocabulary alongside personal integrity.
- **Reconsider impact language:** Notice how often "influence," "platform," and "impact" function as substitutes for "faithfulness." Test them against each other.

## What you can receive from this teacher

This is not a hypothetical or rhetorical question. Real goods are being offered through this stream:

- Genuine biblical faithfulness, often transmitted with great care for the text
- Evangelistic warmth that really does communicate care for persons
- Accessible teaching that has formed real disciples in real households
- Moral seriousness that resists both permissiveness and performative religiosity
- Missional conviction that refuses comfortable insularity

Receive these goods. Receive them with gratitude. The formation considerations above are not reasons to dismiss the teacher; they are invitations to allow the good you have received to deepen further into covenantal maturity.

## Closing note

This profile illustrates one possible blend of strengths and drifts. Most real teachers and traditions are similarly complex mixtures. The goal of any Berean analysis is not to produce a clean verdict, but to increase gratitude for real goods, awareness of real pressures, and clarity about what a more covenantal pattern would require.
