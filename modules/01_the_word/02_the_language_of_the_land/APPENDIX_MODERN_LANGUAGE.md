# Appendix — How Modern Language Re-Trains Us

**Status:** Draft (reference) | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields | **Last updated:** 2026-08-01

---

> This is a reference appendix, not part of the main path. Learn the language of the land first ([Hebrew Word Pictures](/the-word/the-language-of-the-land/hebrew-word-pictures), [Thinking in Blocks](/the-word/the-language-of-the-land/thinking-in-blocks)); this page is here for when you want to notice the pull of your own tongue by contrast.

Once you have begun to hear how the Bible's own language thinks — concrete, embodied, covenantal — you start to notice how far modern speech has drifted from it. We now describe the deepest things in borrowed vocabularies: corporate, therapeutic, consumer, and technological. These words are not neutral. Language tutors perception. A church that constantly speaks of itself in corporate terms will slowly *imagine* itself as a corporation. A believer who speaks of the self in therapeutic terms will slowly imagine the spiritual life as mainly the management of feelings.

This appendix is a diagnostic aid, not a set of forbidden words. Most people speak this way without any ill intent, simply because it is the water we swim in (see [The Cultural Currents](/the-word/knowing-your-own-way/the-cultural-currents)). The aim is gentle noticing, and the recovery of speech that fits a covenantal, embodied, relational frame.

## Where the drift comes from

Modern Christian speech borrows from four main vocabularies, each carrying its own hidden assumptions:

- **Corporate / managerial** — brand, scale, strategy, pipeline, stakeholder, optimization. Imports the logic of the firm into the life of the church.
- **Therapeutic** — safe space, my peace, validation, authenticity, healing journey. Reframes the spiritual life as emotional self-management.
- **Consumer** — experience, options, fit, upgrade, needs met. Turns worship, church, and neighbor into products for the self.
- **Expressive / platform** — my truth, my brand, visibility, my purpose, reinvention. Locates the self in performance and audience.

None of these is evil in itself. Each becomes a problem when it quietly becomes the *default* grid through which God, church, self, and hope are imagined.

## A dictionary of quiet substitutions

A selection, offered as examples rather than an exhaustive list. The point is not to police vocabulary but to make the underlying frame *audible*, and to offer speech that better fits the land of the text.

| Worldly phrase | The frame underneath | Covenantal alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Build the brand | Consumer / corporate identity | Cultivate a faithful communal witness |
| Scale the ministry | Technocratic rationalism | Strengthen the work without outrunning people |
| The church needs a strategy refresh | Corporate institutionalism | This congregation needs renewed faithfulness and wisdom |
| Protect my truth | Expressive individualism | Speak truthfully before God and neighbor |
| I need to live my purpose | Expressive individualism | I need to receive and walk in my calling |
| My identity is my story | Narrative individualism | My identity is received within God's story |
| We need a safe space above all else | Therapeutic anthropology | We need truthful, patient, and compassionate community |
| I'm curating my spiritual journey | Consumer / expressive | I am being formed in a covenant way of life |
| The church should meet my needs | Consumerism | I belong to a people where we bear one another's burdens |
| Optimize the discipleship funnel | Technocratic rationalism | Shepherd people patiently toward maturity |
| I need to protect my peace | Therapeutic inwardness | I need to pursue peace with truth and wisdom |
| We need a platform | Influence culture | We need trustworthy presence and testimony |
| The right side of history | Progressivist utopianism | Faithfulness before God across history |
| Our people are stakeholders | Corporate institutionalism | Our people are members of a covenant community |
| The church is rising / finding her voice | Romantic abstraction | These congregations are being called to renewed faithfulness |

## The two personifications of "the church"

One drift is common enough to name on its own: the habit of speaking of "the Church" as an abstract entity with needs, strategies, and momentum of its own — whether in a *corporate* key ("the church must scale," "protect the brand," "the ministry pipeline is healthy") or a *romantic* key ("the Church is awakening to her destiny," "the Church is rising"). Both, in different registers, detach the word from its concrete referents: actual congregations, elders, households, saints, neighbors, shared obligations, and worshiping people. The corrective is the same in both cases — reattach the speech to embodied realities, and ask not first "what does the institution need?" but "what faithful, truthful, patient care do these actual people need under God?"

## Using this well

The cure is not cynicism about language, and certainly not a new legalism that polices every word. It is the patient recovery of embodied, covenantal speech — which grows most naturally not by scolding but by immersion in the language of the land. The more you learn to think in *dabar* and *hesed* and *shalom*, the more the borrowed vocabularies will start to sound thin to you on their own. Learn the true tongue, and you will hear the substitutes for what they are.
