# Two Major Distortions

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields

---

This document treats two especially common language distortions around the word **church**.

## A. Corporate / Programmatic Personification of "Church"

### What it is

This distortion speaks of the church as though it were primarily an institution, brand, organization, or machine with needs, goals, strategies, and self-preserving momentum independent of actual saints, households, congregations, and embodied practices.

### Philosophical roots

- technocratic rationalism
- corporate institutionalism
- consumerism
- managerial modernity

### Common symptoms

- "The church needs to scale."
- "The church must protect the brand."
- "The church should streamline the customer experience."
- "The ministry pipeline is healthy."

### Why it distorts

These expressions may arise from practical concerns, but they quietly relocate the center of gravity from people, worship, discipline, sacraments, households, and presence to institutional continuity and performance.

### Covenantal correction

Name embodied realities again:

- congregations,
- elders,
- households,
- saints,
- neighbors,
- shared obligations,
- worshiping people.

Ask not first, "What does the institution need?" but "What kind of faithful, truthful, patient care do these people need under God?"

## B. Romantic / Idealistic Personification of "Church"

### What it is

This distortion personifies the church as a mystical ideal, a purified collective self, or an abstract bride-image detached from concrete congregational weakness, discipline, and ordinary life.

### Philosophical roots

- idealism
- romanticism
- expressive collectivism
- abstraction inherited through Latinized and modern devotional language

### Common symptoms

- "The Church is awakening to her destiny."
- "The Church must become beautiful again."
- "The Church is rising."
- "The Church is finding her voice."

### Why it distorts

This language can sound spiritual, but it often bypasses repentance, local faithfulness, and the hard work of embodied life. It turns the church into an ideal object of imagination instead of a concrete people under discipline and grace.

### Covenantal correction

Reattach speech to concrete life:

- actual congregations,
- ordinary worship,
- mutual care,
- confession,
- discipline,
- hospitality,
- endurance.

The church is not less than Christ’s bride, but the bridal image should deepen embodied fidelity rather than float above it.

## Shared pastoral caution

Both distortions can be used by sincere leaders who are trying to do good. The issue is not bad intention; it is formative drift. The cure is not cynicism about language but disciplined recovery of embodied, covenantal speech.
