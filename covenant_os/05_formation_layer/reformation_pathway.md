# Re-formation Pathway

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields | **Last updated:** 2026-08-01

---

## Why this document exists

Diagnosis is not the goal of The Word. Reformation is.

The four lower pathways — the governing frame, the diagnostic engine, the Berean Tool, and the language module — exist to make something visible: the mixed philosophical inheritance that shapes how a person sees God, self, neighbor, and hope. But seeing clearly is only the beginning. A diagnosis that never becomes a re-formed life is just a more sophisticated form of criticism, and criticism without renewal hardens into the very suspicion this module warns against.

The Re-formation Pathway is the hinge. It takes what the diagnostic work reveals and turns it toward repentance, renewal, and embodied practice. It is the bridge from *what is operating beneath my seeing* to *how I am actually being formed into a covenant person* — and from there, into the practices that carry formation week by week and season by season.

This layer answers the practical question left open by everything below it: **Now that I can see the distortion, how do I walk out of it?**

---

## The shape of biblical re-formation

The New Testament does not describe change as self-improvement, optimization, or the accumulation of better ideas. It describes it as a threefold movement: *put off* the old, *be renewed* in the mind, *put on* the new (Ephesians 4:22–24; Colossians 3:9–10; Romans 12:2). This is not a technique. It is the ordinary grammar of dying and rising with Christ, worked out in the concrete details of thought, speech, habit, and shared life.

The pathway keeps that grammar in three movements. Each corresponds to work already done in the lower pathways, and each reaches forward into an embodied practice.

```text
Put off  →  Renew the mind  →  Put on
  ↑                                 |
  |_________ practiced in __________|
        Come to the Table
        Harvest & Hymn
```

The loop is not linear and finished. It is a returning cycle, walked again in each category, each season, each stage of life. In Hebraic terms this is *teshuvah* — turning, returning — not a single decision but a direction of life.

## Movement 1 — Put off

**Put off false gods, false stories, false speech, and false habits.**

Putting off begins where the diagnostic engine ends. Having worked through the seven categories — for yourself first, as the Berean Tool insists — you can now name specific inheritances that have bent your imagination away from covenant. This is not a vague resolution to "do better." It is particular.

The work of putting off asks, category by category:

- **Metaphysics** — Where have I treated creation as neutral stuff, system, or market rather than as gift and covenant theater?
- **Epistemology** — Where have I let detached analysis, private feeling, or reflexive suspicion sit in the seat that belongs to revelation and communal discernment?
- **Anthropology** — Where have I imagined myself as a sovereign chooser, a fragile wounded self, or a consumer, rather than an image-bearer called to communion and stewardship?
- **Ethics** — Where has faithfulness been quietly replaced by safety, efficiency, or visible success?
- **Social/Political** — Where have I spoken of "the Church" as a brand or machine and lost the actual congregation, household, and neighbor?
- **Identity/Meaning** — Where have I built meaning out of platform, curation, or self-authorship rather than receiving it within God's story?
- **Eschatology** — Where has my hope been relocated onto progress, upgrade, or personal healing instead of resurrection?

Putting off is confession before it is correction. It names the false god without pretending the person who served it was a villain — including when that person is you. The posture is the same one the whole module requires: truthful, gentle, unhurried. Repentance, not self-contempt. The aim is to set something down, not to flog yourself for having carried it.

A caution belongs here. Putting off is not the same as throwing away every good thing an inherited tradition gave you. The tradition map and the philosophy modules both insist that rival streams carry real gifts alongside their drifts. Reformation keeps the gift and sets down the distortion. It does not burn the house to kill the moth.

## Movement 2 — Renew the mind

**Return to covenant truth, Scripture, memory, and rightly ordered imagination.**

Putting something down leaves a space. If that space is not filled with covenant truth, the old pattern returns — often in a subtler form. Renewal is the deliberate re-furnishing of the imagination.

This movement draws directly on the governing frame and the language module. Renewing the mind means:

- **Returning to the covenantal contrast.** For each category where you named a drift, sit with the covenantal answer in [covenantal_philosophy.md](../01_foundational_layer/covenantal_philosophy.md) and the category files. Not as information to file, but as a truth to be re-shaped by. Ask: *What would it mean to actually see this way?*
- **Retraining speech.** Because language is the operating system of the imagination, renewal is partly a matter of words. The [worldly vs. covenantal dictionary](../04_language_module/worldly_vs_covenantal_dictionary.md) is a renewal tool, not just a diagnostic one. When you catch yourself reaching for "build the brand," "protect my peace," or "the right side of history," the covenantal alternative is not merely more accurate — it slowly reforms what you can imagine. Changed speech is not cosmetic; it is catechesis.
- **Recovering memory.** Covenant renewal is historical. It works through Scripture read aloud, through the testimony of the saints, through liturgy that carries the community's memory forward. The mind is renewed less by novelty than by re-inhabiting what was given — the story into which you were baptized.
- **Re-ordering the imagination Hebraically.** The governing frame is relational, embodied, apostolic, and historical. Renewal moves thought back toward the concrete: bodies, tables, seasons, places, obligations, and names, rather than abstractions floating free of any actual congregation or household.

Renewal is not a single insight. It is repeated return. The mind is renewed the way a field is kept — not once, but every season.

## Movement 3 — Put on

**Practice covenantal habits, truthful language, embodied obedience, and communal faithfulness.**

Renewal that never becomes practice remains an idea, and ideas do not form people. Character is formed by what is repeated, embodied, and shared. This is why the Re-formation Pathway does not end in the mind. It ends at a table and in a season.

Putting on is the movement from *seeing rightly* to *living covenantally*, and it is inescapably communal. You cannot put on covenant identity alone, because covenant is by definition a bond between persons. This is the point at which The Word hands formation over to its practices — and, further downstream, to the household and the community.

Putting on happens through:

- **Covenant habits.** Small, repeated, embodied acts — daily Scripture, honest confession, hospitality, keeping promises, showing up — that train the affections over time. Formation is cumulative. It is the long obedience that consumerism finds unattractive and covenant finds ordinary.
- **Truthful language in practice.** Not merely knowing the better phrase, but speaking it in real conversation, correspondence, and prayer until it becomes native.
- **The two anchoring practices of The Word:**
  - [**Come to the Table**](./come_to_the_table/README.md) — the weekly household practice where provision becomes formation: a passage read aloud, heard, discussed, and prayed, at an actual table, across the whole range of ages. This is where "put on" happens week by week, in the most ordinary and most formative place in a household's life.
  - [**Harvest & Hymn**](./harvest_and_hymn/README.md) — the quarterly, seasonal community gathering where households bring shared meal, creative offering, and testimony. This is where formation moves outward from the household table into the wider community, under the ordering text of 1 Corinthians 14:26.

Putting on is the point where The Word stops being a body of content and becomes a way of life.

---

## The loop returns

The three movements are not a staircase climbed once. They are a cycle, walked again and again:

1. **Put off** — identify the false god (diagnosis becomes confession)
2. **Return to covenant** — renew the imagination (truth re-furnishes the space)
3. **Walk in the way** — practice the covenant life (renewal becomes habit and shared life)

Each turn through the loop goes deeper. A drift you named in the metaphysics category this year will look different when you return to it after a season of practice. The Berean self-assessment is meant to be repeated, not completed. Reformation is *teshuvah* as a settled direction — a life that keeps turning back toward covenant — not a problem solved and closed.

---

## Where this pathway leads

The Re-formation Pathway is the last movement of The Word, but it is not the end of the formation it begins. It reaches past the boundary of this module.

Reformed imagination does not stay in the head; it reorders a home. The covenant person formed at the table begins to see labor, provision, land, and hospitality differently — which is the work of **The Household**. And rightly ordered households, reaching outward toward one another, begin to form the shared and public life that is the work of **The Compact**.

```text
The Word → forms imagination
    ↓
The Household → households form communities
    ↓
The Compact
```

The re-formation of the imagination is the seed. The household is the soil. The community is the harvest. This pathway is where the seed is put in the ground.

---

## A closing posture

Everything in The Word has aimed at this: not clever diagnosis, not a more refined ability to spot what is wrong in others, but a re-formed people who see, speak, and live covenantally. Diagnosis that does not become reformation curdles into pride. Reformation that does not begin in confession collapses into performance.

The way through is the oldest way there is. Put off. Be renewed. Put on. Turn, and keep turning. And do it not alone, but at a table, in a season, among a people, under God.
