## Purpose

Come to the Table is a liturgical and relational formation practice for community gathering. It creates a structured, safe, and hopeful space in which a community brings what is actually true — including failure, grief, unresolved conflict, and unspoken gratitude — into the open.

The practice is not a meeting format or a discussion template. It is a formation practice: a repeated, embodied rhythm that, over time, shapes a community's capacity for honesty, care, and recommitment to one another.

## What belongs here

- Variant formats for different community sizes and contexts (one-on-one, small group, household, larger gathering)
- Facilitator notes: how to create the conditions for genuine honesty, how to hold difficulty without rushing to resolution, how to close the practice well
- Seasonal and occasional adaptations
- Connection to the Diagnostic Engine (Layer 2): how the practice surfaces beliefs and patterns in a relational context
- Connection to the Language Module (Layer 4): the specific language the practice uses and why those words were chosen

## The practice at a glance

Come to the Table is organized around four movements:

1. **Arrival** — participants are welcomed and the space is set. The facilitator names the purpose and the ground rules.
2. **Examination** — participants bring what is true: what they are carrying, what they have done or left undone, what they are grateful for, what remains unresolved.
3. **Response** — the community responds with care: acknowledgment, naming, and where appropriate, a word of absolution, encouragement, or challenge.
4. **Recommitment** — the practice closes with a shared statement of intention and a sending.
