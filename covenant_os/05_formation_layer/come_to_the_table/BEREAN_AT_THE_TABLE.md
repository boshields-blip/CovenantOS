# Berean at the Table

> *Loving critique of teaching is not a contradiction of charity. It is a form of it.*

## What this file is

This file describes how and when to bring the Berean Tool ([covenant_os/03_berean_tool/](../../03_berean_tool/)) to the family table as part of Come to the Table. The Berean Tool is an occasional, structured practice within the weekly table rhythm — not the center of it.

This is an important file. Read it before introducing the Berean Tool at the table.

---

## Why loving critique is covenantal

The Bereans of Acts 17 are commended not for passive reception of Paul's teaching, but for receiving it eagerly *and then examining the Scriptures to see if what he said was true*. That is the posture this file aims to cultivate.

A household that never learns to evaluate teaching will absorb whatever is offered without discernment. A household that evaluates teaching *uncharitably* will become suspicious, brittle, and unkind. The goal is neither naïveté nor cynicism. It is covenantal discernment.

This is especially important for the **Apprentice** stage (~15+). The Apprentice is old enough to recognize that not all teaching is equivalent, and young enough that their critical instincts may still be shaped toward warmth rather than contempt.

The Berean Tool at the table teaches:
- That faithful people can be wrong without being faithless.
- That a partial truth is still a truth, even when it is also incomplete.
- That the goal of discernment is formation, not a verdict.
- That we read others the way we hope to be read.

---

## When to use it

The Berean Tool at the table is occasional — perhaps once a month, perhaps less. It is not part of every session. Sessions that use the Berean Tool are longer and should be chosen for moments when the household has capacity.

**Good occasions:**
- After a sermon the family heard together that week
- While a family member is reading a Christian book or following a teacher
- When a child brings home something they heard in youth group or at a church event
- When a popular Christian figure comes up in conversation
- During Year 4 of the canonical arc, when the philosophy categories are explicitly in view

**Not good occasions:**
- When guests are present who are not familiar with the tool or the household's practice (see [GUEST_FAMILY_GUIDE.md](./GUEST_FAMILY_GUIDE.md))
- When Little Ones or Image Bearers are at the table and fully present — keep the discussion brief and simple, or defer to after they have left the table
- When the purpose is to win an argument, confirm an existing opinion, or dismiss someone

## How to use it at the table

The Berean Tool at the table is a simplified version of the full tool described in [03_berean_tool/how_to_use.md](../../03_berean_tool/how_to_use.md). The table version does not produce a full profile. It simply uses the tool's posture and a few of its core questions.

**A simple table structure:**

1. **Name the subject.** *"We heard a sermon this week from [description of teacher or context]. Let's think about it for a few minutes."*
2. **Ask "what is true here?"** Before anything else, name what was good, faithful, or nourishing in what was heard. This is not a formality — it is the real starting place.
3. **Ask "what background shapes this?"** Who is this person? What tradition formed them? What do they tend to emphasize?
4. **Ask "what might be partial or stretched?"** Not "what is wrong" — but "is there something here that feels like a half-truth?" or "is there a category where this teaching seems to lean away from what we have been hearing in Scripture?"
5. **Ask "what would a more covenantal answer look like?"** What does the passage we read tonight say about this same question?
6. **Close with "what can we receive from this?"** The Berean conversation ends with reception, not rejection.

---

## Guardrails

These are not suggestions. They are the rules that keep this practice healthy and safe.

**Never name a person in a shaming way.**
The table is not a tribunal. Naming a teacher by name while expressing contempt models something corrosive, especially for younger children. If a teacher must be named (because the sermon was heard that week), name them as you would want to be named.

**Always ask "what is true here?" before "what is off here?"**
This is not a technique. It is a conviction: partial truths are still truths. A household that only names errors loses the ability to receive genuine goods from teachers they disagree with on some points.

**Adapt to the youngest person at the table.**
If Little Ones or Image Bearers are fully present and engaged, the Berean discussion stays brief, simple, and warm. Do not use the table as a place to work through complex theological disagreement over a toddler's macaroni.

If necessary, save the fuller Berean conversation for after younger children leave the table — for dessert without the little ones, or for an evening conversation later.

**Apprentices lead with questions, not verdicts.**
The Apprentice who leads a Berean moment at the table should ask, not declare. *"I noticed that this teacher seems to say X — does that fit with what we've been reading?"* is formation. *"This teacher is wrong"* is not.

**Mentors model charity, not cleverness.**
An adult who demonstrates sophisticated critique without genuine warmth teaches the wrong lesson. The Mentor's role in a Berean conversation is to show that it is possible to say "I have real concerns here" and also "I am grateful for this person's courage, gifts, or faithfulness in these areas."

**No Berean critique of a guest's tradition or teachers at a guest table.**
See [GUEST_FAMILY_GUIDE.md](./GUEST_FAMILY_GUIDE.md) for the explicit rule here. When another family is at the table, the Berean Tool stays away.

## A sample Apprentice-led Berean moment

The following is a short script showing how an Apprentice might lead a Berean moment at the table. The teacher and context are left generic.

---

*The family has just finished the Notice movement from a session on a passage about identity.*

**Apprentice:** *"Can I bring up something from Sunday? The sermon was about identity, and [the teacher] said something like 'you are defined by what you love most.' I was thinking about that."*

**Host:** *"Sure. What did you hear that was true in that?"*

**Apprentice:** *"I think it's true that what you love shapes you. That part felt right. Augustine says something similar."*

**Host:** *"Good. What tradition is [the teacher] from? What tends to shape how they think about this?"*

**Apprentice (or Host):** *"They come from [tradition]. They tend to emphasize interior life, personal transformation, that kind of thing."*

**Host:** *"Where might that emphasis create a partial picture?"*

**Apprentice:** *"I think the passage we read tonight says something different. It's not 'you are what you love' — it's more like 'you are who you belong to.' Identity is received, not generated."*

**Host:** *"That's worth sitting with. What would be a covenantal way to say what [the teacher] is trying to say?"*

**Apprentice:** *"Something like... what you love is shaped by who you belong to. Start with belonging, then talk about desire."*

**Host:** *"Good. What can we receive from [the teacher] even with that adjustment?"*

**Apprentice:** *"The invitation to pay attention to what you actually love — not just what you say you love. That's worth keeping."*

**Host:** *"Agreed. Let's keep that. Alright — back to Live."*

---

This is brief, warm, specific, and generative. It neither dismisses the teacher nor uncritically accepts the framing. The Apprentice leads with a question, receives what is good, names a possible distortion, offers a covenantal alternative, and ends with gratitude.

That is the posture the table should model.

## Connection to the full Berean Tool

The table version of the Berean conversation is an entry point. For more structured analysis — a fuller profile, a careful category-by-category examination, a written comparison — use the full Berean Tool outside the meal.

The full tool is designed for personal study, small-group work, or individual discernment. The table version is designed for formation in the midst of the meal — brief, accessible, and generative.
