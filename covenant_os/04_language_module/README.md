## Purpose

The Language Module builds and maintains a shared covenantal vocabulary. Language is not neutral: the words a community uses to describe persons, relationships, failure, restoration, and hope shape how it understands and practices covenant. Sloppy language produces sloppy formation. Precise, formative language produces communities that can think and speak clearly about what matters most.

The Language Module does not invent new jargon. It recovers, refines, and defines language that is rooted in the Foundational Layer's first principles and informed by what the Diagnostic Engine has revealed about how language is actually being used — and misused — in formation contexts.

## What belongs here

- **Glossary of covenantal terms:** definitions of key terms with usage notes, common misuses, and the distinctions that matter most
- **Formation language guides:** how to speak about persons (their dignity, their failure, their growth), about community (obligation, care, conflict, repair), and about the practices that form us
- **Language patterns to cultivate:** ways of speaking that reinforce healthy formation — naming things accurately, owning responsibility, celebrating restoration
- **Language patterns to avoid:** ways of speaking that undermine formation — minimizing, blaming, spiritualizing away concrete responsibility, using vague language to avoid difficult truths
- **Vocabulary for the Formation Layer:** the specific language used in Come to the Table, Harvest and Hymn, and other formation practices, defined here so that facilitators and participants have a shared reference

## Connection to lower layers

The Language Module draws on the Foundational Layer (Layer 1) for its root definitions, on the Diagnostic Engine (Layer 2) for insight into how language is actually functioning in formation contexts, and on the Berean Tool (Layer 3) for vocabulary drawn directly from primary sources. It does not reference the Formation Layer.
