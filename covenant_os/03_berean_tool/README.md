## Purpose

The Berean Tool is a scripture and text examination tool. It is named after the Bereans of Acts 17:11, who "received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so." The posture it cultivates is rigorous, humble, and eager: willing to be taught, but insisting on examining the source rather than accepting any claim without verification.

The Berean Tool provides structured approaches to engaging primary sources — chiefly scripture, but also other formative texts that a community has adopted as authoritative or worth sustained examination. It is not a theological commentary or a Bible study curriculum; it is a method for doing the examination well.

## What belongs here

- **Observation-interpretation-application (OIA) templates:** the classic three-movement approach to text examination, adapted for formation contexts
- **Close reading prompts:** questions designed to slow the reader down and notice what the text actually says before rushing to what it means
- **Cross-reference and comparison structures:** tools for holding multiple texts in relationship, noting convergence and tension
- **Source examination guides:** how to evaluate a formative text — its genre, its historical and literary context, its relationship to the Foundational Layer's core commitments
- **Group examination formats:** how to lead a Berean-style examination in a formation group, including roles, timing, and ground rules

## Connection to lower layers

The Berean Tool draws on the Foundational Layer (Layer 1) to understand what it is examining toward, and on the Diagnostic Engine (Layer 2) to note where the text examination reveals beliefs or contradictions the reader brings to the text. It does not reference the Language Module or Formation Layer.
