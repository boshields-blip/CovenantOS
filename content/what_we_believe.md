# What We Believe

**Status:** Draft | **Author:** Brandon O'Shields | **Last updated:** 2026-08-09

---

Everything else on this site is a lens. This page is not.

The Word, the Household, and the Compact all describe a particular way of seeing — Hebraic, covenantal, household-rooted — and I have tried to be honest everywhere that it is one stream among many faithful ones, held to the same standard I ask of every other tradition. But underneath that lens sits something that is not mine to adjust: the confession every orthodox Christian tradition already shares. Before CovenantOS is Hebraic, agrarian, table-centered, or anything else distinctive, it is simply Christian. This page says what that means, plainly, so you never have to guess.

## The Triune God

We believe in one God, eternally existing in three persons — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Not three gods, and not one person wearing three masks, but one God whose very life is relationship. The Father is the source who adopts us into His household. The Son, Jesus Christ, is fully God and fully man — crucified, risen, ascended, and enthroned. The Holy Spirit is not a force or an influence but God Himself, personally present, joining us to Christ and to one another.

This is not an abstract puzzle to be solved. It is the household we are adopted into. Salvation is not merely a transaction settled in the past; it is communion, ongoing, with the Father, through the Son, by the Spirit.

## The gospel: crucified, risen, ascended, and reigning

Jesus Christ died for our sins, exactly as the Scriptures said He would. He rose bodily on the third day — not as a metaphor for spring or a symbol of hope, but up out of an actual grave. He ascended bodily into heaven, and He is, at this very hour, enthroned as our great High Priest, interceding for us.

That last part is the piece most often left out, and it shouldn't be. Hebrews will not let you stop the story at the cross. Jesus took His own blood into the true and heavenly sanctuary and is there now, on our behalf, this moment. We are not saved only because Jesus died. We are saved because Jesus died, rose, ascended, and lives to intercede for us. His nearness to the Father is our access to the Father. His standing is our standing.

## The Spirit who unites us

The Spirit is the one who takes what is true of Jesus and makes it true of us — joining us to His death and resurrection, cleansing and renewing us, sealing our adoption, and knitting believers into households and tables of shared life. He is the reason union with Christ is not a doctrine to admire from a distance but a life we actually share.

## Our hope: a body, a return, a renewed world

We believe Jesus is coming back — bodily, visibly, the same way the disciples watched Him go (Acts 1:9–11). We believe our own bodies will be raised, not discarded. We believe creation itself will be healed rather than abandoned. This is not a chart or a timeline to argue over. It is the shape of Christian hope: not an escape from this world into a disembodied heaven, but the coming of heaven into a world set right.

Every table we gather around now is a small rehearsal of a bigger one still coming — the marriage supper of the Lamb (Revelation 19:6–9). Our households, imperfect as they are, are previews. This hope is why we can grieve honestly and still worship, why we can labor at ordinary things — a field, a trade, a meal — without despair. The story does not end at a grave. It ends at a table, in a renewed world, with God dwelling among His people.

## Baptism: the door

Baptism is not a symbol added on top of belief. It is the doorway into everything above — the place where a person is joined to Christ's death and resurrection, cleansed, and welcomed into the household of God. However your tradition understands its exact form and timing, we affirm what baptism *does*: it marks the place a person stopped standing outside the covenant and started standing inside it.

## A kingdom of priests

Because Jesus is our High Priest, every believer is drawn into a kind of priesthood too — not instead of His, but because of it (1 Peter 2:9). This is not a claim about who may administer any particular rite; it is the ordinary Christian confession that every household can bless, every believer can intercede in Jesus's name, and no Christian's daily faithfulness — teaching a child, welcoming a stranger, reconciling a quarrel — is a small thing. We do not perform sacrifice; Christ already has. We embody its fruit.

## Table fellowship, in brief

The table is where this confession becomes visible, which is why CovenantOS spends so much time there. We will not re-argue that here — see [Come to the Table](/the-word/living-the-covenant/come-to-the-table) for the practice, and [To See](/the-word/walking-the-text/to-see) for the theology of what it means to behold Christ and one another rightly. This page only says why the table matters enough to build a practice around: because the blood of Jesus and the fellowship of His people were never meant to be separated.

## The confession, in one paragraph

The gospel is the good news that Jesus Christ — the eternal Son of God — became human, lived among us, died for our sins, rose bodily from the grave, ascended into heaven, and now intercedes for us as our great High Priest. Through baptism we are joined to His death and resurrection; through the Holy Spirit we are cleansed, adopted into the household of God, and formed into a people who bless and represent Him in ordinary life. His sacrifice fulfills what the Passover lamb foreshadowed and gathers us to His table. We wait for His bodily return and our bodily resurrection, when He will renew all things and dwell with His people forever.

## Where this leaves you

If you hold to this, you already stand on the same ground CovenantOS stands on, whatever tradition handed it to you — Baptist, Catholic, Orthodox, Pentecostal, Anglican, Messianic, or something with no denominational name at all. Everything else here — the household as first church, the table as center, covenant over contract — is what we do with this confession, not a replacement for it. See [The Household-Covenant Tradition](/the-word/the-streams-of-the-traditions/the-household-covenant-tradition) for that lens, named openly, and held to the same honesty this page asks of everyone else.
