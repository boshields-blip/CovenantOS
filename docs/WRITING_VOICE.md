# Writing Voice — Brandon O'Shields

**Purpose:** A reference for drafting in Brandon's voice, so CovenantOS content and personal writing sound like him. Two registers are described below — the *teaching register* (most of the atlas) and the *personal register* (testimony and lament). Edit this file freely; it is meant to be corrected over time.

---

## The two registers

**1. Teaching register** — the voice of most CovenantOS content. Covenantal, Hebraic, embodied, and pastoral. Accessible depth: credible but not academic; concrete over abstract; warm, unhurried, charitable. Names its own lens openly and refuses tribalism. This is the register the modules are written in.

**2. Personal register** — the voice of Brandon's testimony, prayers, and laments. Raw, first-person, confessional, and Christ-drenched. Psalmic. Unafraid of pain, anger, and vulnerability before God. This is the voice of the lament he wrote and of the About page. Use it for anything spoken *as himself*, not *about* a subject.

Both registers share the same spine (below). The difference is heat and nearness: teaching is warm and steady; personal is raw and close.

---

## The shared spine

- **Christ crucified and risen at the center.** Everything comes back to Jesus — the pierced one, the risen one, the Creator and Redeemer. Cross, resurrection, the Lord's Supper (eating his flesh, drinking his blood), living sacrifice, and the sure hope of his return "upon the clouds" all belong here.
- **Scripture woven, not cited.** Biblical language is carried as his own — Zechariah's pierced one, John 6, Romans 12, Acts 1, the Psalms — rather than dropped in with chapter-and-verse. Allusion over citation.
- **Seeing / eyes opened.** His native motif. Sight breaks in; "the eyes of my heart were opened." Formation is learning to see. (This is the spine of the whole atlas, and it comes from him.)
- **Covenant, household, land, table, neighbor.** The concrete furniture of the faith. Embodied, agrarian, relational, place-based.
- **Question, don't deconstruct.** Honest, even hard questioning is good; tearing down one's tradition is not. Refine; don't demolish. Keep the gift, set down the distortion.
- **Unity across traditions.** Appreciates every stream; the covenant-household way fits *within* them, not over against them.

---

## Signature devices (strongest in the personal register)

- **The interrogative pilgrimage.** He thinks in cascades of raw questions, and the questions *are* the movement. He does not explain his way to truth; he asks his way there until sight breaks in. ("Why do I…? Does he not understand…? Who is this one…?")
- **Turn-lines as hinges.** Short refrains set apart on their own line mark each turning: *"And then my eyes were opened." / "And then my heart was broken."* Liturgical, psalmic, and load-bearing.
- **The lament-to-worship arc.** He begins in complaint — even accusation against God — passes through conviction of his own sin and being undone by grace, and lands in worship and hope. The honesty of the descent is what makes the ascent ring true.
- **Direct address and plain speech.** "Oh Lord, help me." Anglo-Saxon, plain, KJV/ESV cadence. Not fancy — weighty.
- **Parallelism and repetition.** Sentences echo and build. Rhythm carries the meaning.

---

## Diction and rhythm

- Prefer short and medium sentences with biblical cadence; let parallelism and repetition build.
- Prefer plain, concrete, embodied words (blood, bread, soil, table, spear, field) over abstractions.
- First person and direct address are welcome in the personal register.
- Contractions are fine; keep it human, not stiff.

## Avoid

- Corporate, managerial, therapeutic, or consumer jargon (brand, scale, optimize, my truth, my peace, engagement) — the very speech CovenantOS diagnoses.
- Over-abstraction and floating idealism; keep it grounded in bodies, land, and Christ.
- Sanitizing the personal register into blandness. Its power is its rawness; keep the heat.
- Over-hedging and over-explaining. Trust the reader; let the turn do the work.

## A note on palatability

Brandon's personal writing is intense and can be hard for some readers to follow. The remedy is not to dull it but to *frame* it: a short orienting line before a lament, so a reader knows they are entering a movement from pain to worship, lets the rawness land without losing them. Preserve the voice; add a doorway.

---

## Touchstone

The lament beginning *"What is this rending of my soul?"* — with its march of questions, its turn-lines, and its arc from accusation to worship — is the clearest example of the personal register. When in doubt, read it and match its heat and honesty.
